# Original Short 06 · Don't think of a white bear

**Hook on screen (first 2 s):** "Don't think of a white bear"
**Length:** 107 words, about 43 seconds at Josh's pace (Short 05 ran at about 2.5 words a second).

## Why this should travel (the formula from Shorts 04 and 05)

- **The viewer fails in the first second.** "Don't think of a white bear." It's impossible, so everyone does it and
  is hooked by their own brain. Like the tickle Short, the viewer takes part.
- **The gap is named straight away:** "Too late, right?" Then the real experiment explains why.
- **A sound and image people remember:** students ringing a bell every time the bear popped up. *Ding. Ding.
  Ding.*
- **A re-hook halfway through:** the bear came back *even more* once they were allowed to think about it.
- **It reaches the viewer's own life:** "Same with an ex. Or that awkward thing you said." This is what gets
  comments and shares.
- **A fix they can use, with a weird detail:** pick a different thought on purpose. In the study, it was a red car.
- **A loop:** "…don't think of a white bear." runs straight into "Don't think of a white bear." The replay makes
  the viewer fail again.

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs)

```
Don't think of a white bear. Don't. Too late, right? In nineteen eighty-seven, psychologist Daniel Wegner tried this on students. They talked out loud for five minutes. One rule. Don't think of a white bear. Every time it popped up, they rang a bell. Ding. Ding. Ding. And when the rule ended, the bear came back even more. Fighting a thought keeps it alive. Same with an ex. Or that awkward thing you said. But one trick helped. Pick a different thought on purpose. In the study, it was a red car. So picture a red car. And whatever you do, don't think of a white bear.
```

Voice: Josh, same settings. Read "Don't." as a firm warning, then "Too late, right?" with a grin. Make each
"Ding." short and bright, with a beat between them.
