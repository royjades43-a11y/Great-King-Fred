# Original Short 05 · Why you can't tickle yourself

**Hook on screen (first 2 s):** "Try to tickle yourself"
**Length:** 105 words, about 39 seconds at Josh's pace (Short 04 ran at 2.6 words a second).

## Why this should travel (built on what worked in Short 04)

Short 04 worked because it was:
- instantly relatable
- a surprising culprit straight away
- one simple explanation
- a seamless loop

This one keeps all four and adds a fifth lever:
- **The viewer acts in the first second:** "Try to tickle yourself. Right now." People actually try it. That's
  attention you can't fake, and it stops the swipe.
- **A curiosity gap straight away:** "Nothing, right? There's a reason."
- **A weird, memorable detail:** scientists built a *tickle robot*. It's the kind of fact people repeat to
  friends, which means shares.
- **A second reveal halfway through:** the delay ("a fifth of a second… suddenly, it tickled more") re-hooks the
  viewer where Shorts usually lose people.
- **One sticky line to take away:** "A tickle without surprise is just a touch."
- **A loop that also gets acted on:** "Bet you're about to try it again." runs straight into "Try to tickle
  yourself." The replay is the viewer trying again. That's the rewatch behaviour the Shorts feed rewards.

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs)

```
Try to tickle yourself. Right now. Go on. Nothing, right? There's a reason. In the late nineteen nineties, neuroscientist Sarah-Jayne Blakemore and her team built a tickle robot. People moved a handle, and the robot stroked their palm with soft foam. When it copied their movement instantly, it barely tickled. Then they added a tiny delay. A fifth of a second. Suddenly, it tickled more. Your brain predicts every move you make. When it sees a touch coming, it turns the feeling down. Your own hand can't surprise you. And a tickle without surprise is just a touch. Bet you're about to try it again.
```

Voice: Josh, same settings. Read "Go on." and "Nothing, right?" playfully, with a beat after each, as if you're
watching the viewer try it. Use a lighter, amused tone on "a tickle robot".
