# Original Short 04 · Blame the door

**Hook on screen (first 2 s):** "You walk in… and forget why"
**Length:** 99 words, about 35 seconds at Josh's pace.

## Why this should travel (applied research)

Shorts guides agree on a few things (vendor sources, so treat the numbers loosely, but the advice is
consistent):
- the first 1–2 seconds decide whether viewers swipe ([ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-decoded.html))
- an ending that flows back into the opening invites replays ([Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/youtube-shorts-hooks-and-curiosity-loops-that-explode-your-views/))
- the retention graph, not how clever the hook sounded, is the judge ([JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices/))

Applied here:
- **Instant recognition in under 2 seconds:** "You walk into a room. And you forget why." Almost everyone has done it
  this week.
- **A surprising culprit straight away:** "Blame the door." It's a curiosity gap the viewer has to close.
- **Proof in three short lines,** then a second reveal halfway through ("Here's why…") to re-hook the viewer where
  Shorts usually lose people.
- **One image that sticks:** the brain stores your day in chapters, and a doorway is a page break.
- **A kind takeaway** people want to share or tag a friend with.
- **A loop ending:** "…the next time you walk into a room." runs straight back into "You walk into a room." A
  replay feels seamless.
- **A visual change every 1–2 seconds,** and the hook text holds for the feed preview.

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs)

```
You walk into a room. And you forget why. Blame the door. In twenty eleven, psychologist Gabriel Radvansky had people carry objects from table to table. Some walked through a doorway. Others walked the same distance inside one room. The doorway group forgot more. Here's why. Your brain stores your day in chapters. A doorway is a page break. New room, new chapter. And the reason you came in? It stayed in the last one. So it's not your memory failing. It's your brain turning the page. Be kind to yourself the next time you walk into a room.
```

Voice: Josh, same settings as the long videos. Leave a small beat after "And you forget why." and before "Blame
the door."

## Sources

| Line | Source |
| --- | --- |
| People carried objects between tables, in virtual and real rooms; walking through a doorway led to more forgetting than walking the same distance in one room | Radvansky, Krawietz & Tamplin (2011), *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* 64(8), 1632–1645 ([BPS summary](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-walking-through-doorway-increases-forgetting)) |
| "Chapters" / "page break" | The authors' explanation: a doorway is an event boundary that starts a new memory episode. "Chapters" is our plain-English version. |
| Caveat | A 2021 replication attempt found no doorway effect in most conditions (McFadyen et al., *BMC Psychology*), so the script stays with "the doorway group forgot more" in this experiment and doesn't call it a universal law. |
