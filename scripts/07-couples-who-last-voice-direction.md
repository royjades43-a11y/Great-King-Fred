# Voice direction: "The Psychology of Couples Who Last"

A line-by-line reading guide, in the same format as videos 5 and 6 (see `scripts/05-always-late-voice-direction.md`
for the 12 rules and the full legend). It works for a human narrator, ElevenLabs or AI Studio (use each
section's **Direction** line as the style prompt), and for our free AI voice through `voice/directed.py`.

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **word** | stress it |
| `\|` / `\|\|` | short / long beat |
| ↘ / ↗ | land down / lift (a genuine question) |
| (brisk) (slower) (land it) (lean in) (warm) (smile) (light) (character) | how to deliver the line |

Numbers are written the way they are spoken.

## Energy map

```
energy  5 |
        4 |  *       *        *              *      *
        3 |     *  *    *  *     *   *   *       *     *
        2 |                                *              *
        1 |

            hook  lab ratio horsemen 69%  bird  good-news fix twist end
```

Overall this is the warmest video so far: tender, a little playful, never preachy. The Four Horsemen
are the most serious part; the bird is the emotional centre; the ending is soft.

## 1 · Hook (0:00–0:40)
**Direction:** intimate and gentle, like describing a quiet morning. A touch of mystery on "might say
more about your relationship". Energy 4.

(warm) Your partner is looking out the **window**. |
(character) They say, | "Look at that **bird**." ||
(light) That's it. | Nothing **important**. ||
(lean in) What you do in the next few seconds | might say more about your relationship | than your last big **fight**. ||
(smile) Sound **strange**? ↗ ||
Most people would say good couples **communicate** better, | or fight **less**. |
(lean in) That's not quite what the **cameras** showed. ||
Researchers have spent **decades** watching couples, | live, | with cameras and heart monitors. |
And they found that couples who last | aren't the ones who never **fight**. ||
By the end of this video, you'll know what they do **instead**. |
(lean in) And the **ten-second** habit | that predicted who stayed **together**. ||

## 2 · The Love Lab (0:40–2:05)
**Direction:** a storyteller with a curious edge, like describing a strange experiment. Slow down on
"They waited. For years." Fair and calm on the critics' line. Energy 3.

In the nineteen-eighties and nineties, two psychologists, | John **Gottman** and Robert **Levenson**, | did something **unusual**. |
They studied couples the way biologists study **animals**. ||
(land it) By **watching**. ||
Couples came into the lab and talked about their **day**, | and about a topic they always **fought** about. |
The researchers recorded every **word**, | every facial **expression**, | and every jump in **heart rate**. ||
(smile) Later, Gottman even built a lab that looked like a small **apartment**, | with a view of a **park**. |
Couples spent a whole day there, | just **living**, | while the cameras rolled. ||
Then the researchers did the **important** part. ||
(slower) They **waited**. | For **years**. ||
They checked back to see which couples were still **together**. |
And they looked for the patterns that had been there | from the **start**. ||
Gottman's team reported that they could predict which couples would divorce | with over **ninety percent** accuracy. ||
Some researchers have argued those numbers are less impressive than they **sound**. |
The models were fitted to couples the team had **already** studied. |
But the patterns themselves have **held up**. ||
(lean in) One of the clearest came from just the first **three minutes**. |
In a nineteen ninety-nine study, the team filmed one hundred and twenty-four newlywed couples | having **one** argument. |
The opening minutes **alone** | predicted who'd still be married | six years later. ||
(land it) The way a fight **starts** | is usually the way it **ends**. ||
(brisk) So what **else** did they find? ↗ ||

## 3 · The magic ratio (2:05–2:55)
**Direction:** clear and confident, a little bright. Make "Five to one." a clean beat. Energy 3.

The next one was a **number**. |
During a fight, stable couples didn't **avoid** negativity. |
(lean in) They **balanced** it. ||
For every negative moment, | a criticism, | an eye-roll, | a sharp tone, | they had about **five** positive ones. |
(smile) A **touch**. | A **joke**. | A "you're **right**." | A smile in the middle of an argument. ||
(land it) Five | to **one**. ||
Couples heading for divorce were closer to **one** to one. |
(slower) Sometimes **less**. ||
In other words, a happy couple can have plenty of **fights**. |
(smile) They just have a lot more of **everything else**. ||
So the question isn't **whether** you fight. |
It's what **else** happens while you're fighting. |
(lean in) And some negative moments | are far more **dangerous** than others. ||

## 4 · The Four Horsemen (2:55–4:40)
**Direction:** the most serious part. A steady countdown; each "Number…" is a clean new start. Quotes
in a light character voice. Contempt is slower and lower. The antidotes lift the mood: warm and practical.
Energy 3, dipping to 2 on contempt, back to 3 on the antidotes.

Gottman found four behaviors that predicted the **end**. |
He called them the Four **Horsemen**. ||
(brisk) Number **one**: | **criticism**. |
Not complaining about something you **did**. | Attacking who you **are**. |
(character) A complaint says, | "The trash is still **here**." |
(character) Criticism says, | "You **never** think about anyone but yourself." ||
(brisk) Number **two**: | **defensiveness**. |
Answering every complaint with an **excuse**, | or a **counter-attack**. |
(character) "Well, **you're** the one who..." ||
(brisk) Number **three**: | **stonewalling**. |
Shutting **down**. | Going **silent**. | Turning into a **wall**. |
(warm) If you've watched our video on people who go quiet in a fight, | you'll know this one. |
Very often, it's a body that's **overwhelmed**, | not a heart that doesn't **care**. |
But over time, | it still does **damage**. ||
(slower) And number **four**, | the worst of **all**: ||
(land it) **contempt**. ||
**Mockery**. | **Sarcasm**. | **Eye-rolling**. | Talking **down**. |
(character) It says, | "I'm **better** than you." ||
Gottman called contempt the single best predictor of **divorce**. |
(lean in) And in his research, | it seemed to reach the **body** too. |
He reported that partners on the receiving end of contempt | had more **infectious illnesses** | over the next four years. ||
(warm) The good news | is that each horseman has an **antidote**. |
Instead of criticism, | start **gently**. |
(character) "I feel stressed when the dishes pile up," | not "You're so **lazy**." |
Instead of defensiveness, | take responsibility for even a **small** part. |
Instead of stonewalling, | take a real **break**. | **Twenty minutes** to calm your body, | then come **back**. |
And instead of contempt, | build a habit of **appreciation**. |
(warm) Notice what they do **right**, | and **say** it. ||
But even the antidotes | aren't the whole **story**. |
(lean in) Here's the part nobody **expects**. ||

## 5 · Most fights never end (4:40–5:25)
**Direction:** a reveal, then relief. Light on the list of familiar fights (the viewer should smile in
recognition). Energy 3.

(slower) Happy couples | don't solve most of their **problems**. ||
According to Gottman, | about **sixty-nine percent** of relationship conflicts | are **perpetual**. |
They never really go **away**. ||
(light) The **money** argument. | The messy **kitchen**. | Whose family you visit for the **holidays**. ||
They come from real differences in **personality**, | and those don't **disappear**. |
(smile) As one therapist put it, | choosing a partner means choosing a set of **problems** | you'll be living with for years. ||
Couples who last don't **win** these arguments. |
They learn to **live** with them, | with **humor**, | and without **contempt**. |
(warm) They stop trying to **win**, | and start trying to **understand**. ||
So if it isn't about fixing every problem, | what **is** it about? ↗ ||
(land it) It's about the **bird**. ||

## 6 · The bird (5:25–6:45)
**Direction:** the emotional centre. Tender and clear. Three answers read as three small scenes. The
86 / 33 reveal is slow; "Just looking at the bird." is the softest line in the video. Energy 3–4.

Gottman calls them **bids**. |
A bid is any small attempt to **connect**. |
They happen all day long, | and most of them are **tiny**. ||
(character) "Look at that **bird**." | "Guess what happened at **work**." | (light) A **sigh**. | A hand on your **shoulder**. ||
And every bid gets one of **three** answers. ||
(smile) You can turn **toward** it. | (character) "Oh wow, | what kind **is** it?" ||
You can turn **away**. | You keep scrolling, | and say **nothing**. ||
Or you can turn **against** it. | (character) "Can't you see I'm **busy**?" ||
(warm) Turning away can feel **harmless**. | You were just busy. |
But to the person reaching out, | silence can sound like | "you don't **matter**." ||
In one study, Gottman followed newlywed couples for **six** years. |
(slower) The couples who were still married | had turned toward each other's bids | about **eighty-six percent** of the time. ||
(lean in) The couples who divorced? ↗ ||
(land it) About **thirty-three** percent. ||
That's the ten-second habit I **promised** you. |
Not grand **gestures**. | Not perfect **communication**. ||
(land it) Just looking at the **bird**. ||
(warm) Each time you turn toward a bid, you're saying, | "I'm **here**. | You **matter**." |
And each time you turn away, | a little bit of **trust** leaks out. ||
(lean in) But it wasn't over **yet**. ||

## 7 · The good news test (6:45–7:45)
**Direction:** lighter and more playful again. Act out the three replies to "I got the promotion!":
flat, worried, delighted. The obstacle course is funny; enjoy it. Energy 4.

(brisk) Here's one most people never **think** about. |
We all know a partner should support you when things go **wrong**. |
But psychologist Shelly Gable found that how they react when things go **right** | may matter even **more**. ||
Imagine you come home and say, | (character) "I got the **promotion**!" ||
(light) Your partner could say, | "Nice," | and keep watching **TV**. |
(character) They could say, | "Won't that mean longer **hours**?" |
(smile) Or they could light up and ask, | "Tell me **everything**. | What did your boss **say**?" ||
Only that last kind of response, | interested and **enthusiastic**, | was linked to more **trust**, | **intimacy** | and **satisfaction**. ||
Celebrating someone is a **skill**. | (smile) And most of us are a bit **lazy** at it. |
When did you last react to good news | like it was the **best** thing you'd heard all week? ↗ ||
(brisk) Then there's **boredom**. |
In two thousand, psychologist Arthur Aron gave couples a **strange** task. |
(light) Some were tied together at the **wrist** and **ankle**, | and had to cross a padded obstacle course | carrying a **pillow**. |
Others did something **dull**. ||
After just **seven** minutes, | the couples who did the new, slightly **ridiculous** task | reported feeling **better** about their relationship. ||
(warm) Novelty, it seems, | brings back a little of the early **spark**. |
(smile) Your usual restaurant won't do it. | Something **new** will. ||

## 8 · What to do with this (7:45–8:25)
**Direction:** a warm coach. A quick, clear list; each item lands. Energy 4, settling to 3.

(brisk) So what can you actually do **tomorrow**? ↗ ||
Start hard talks **gently**. | The first three minutes **matter**. |
Turn toward the **bids**. | Even the **boring** ones. |
Aim for **five** good moments for every bad one, | especially in a fight. |
Swap contempt for **appreciation**. | Say what you're grateful for, | out **loud**. |
(warm) Celebrate the good news like it **matters**, | because to them, | it **does**. |
Try something **new** together. | It doesn't have to be expensive. | It just has to be **new**. ||
(slower) And don't wait too long to ask for **help**. |
Gottman has said the average couple waits about **six years** with problems | before they get help. |
That's a long time to let things **build**. ||
(smile) And the best part? ↗ | None of this costs **anything**. ||

## 9 · The twist and the ending (8:25–9:00)
**Direction:** the softest, slowest part. Let the last line about the bird land and breathe. The
challenge and CTA are friendly and light. Energy 2, then 3.

(slower) So here's the **twist**. ||
Couples who last | still **fight**. | Most of their problems never go **away**. |
What makes them different | is **tiny**. ||
(warm) They notice when the other person **reaches out**. |
(land it) And they reach **back**. ||
(slower) Love doesn't usually end | with one big **fight**. |
(land it) It fades in the moments | nobody looked at the **bird**. ||

(brisk) So here's your **challenge** for this week. |
Every time someone you love says "look at **this**," | stop | and actually **look**. |
Then come back and tell me what **changed**. ||

(smile) When your partner or a friend says "Look at **that**," | do you **look**? ↗ | Tell me in the comments. |
Which habit should we cover **next**? ↗ | Drop it in the comments. ↘
