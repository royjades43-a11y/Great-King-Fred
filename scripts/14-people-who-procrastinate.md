# Video 14: The Psychology of People Who Procrastinate

**Title:** The Psychology of People Who Procrastinate
**Length:** 1471 words. That's about 8:45 at Josh's pace (video 13 ran at 2.81 words a second), so it's over 8:00
and mid-roll ads are possible.

## Why this title and topic

- **Search plus series:** "why do I procrastinate" is one of the most searched psychology questions there is. The
  title keeps the "The Psychology of People Who…" pattern (42 characters, fits on phones, no question mark).
- **Almost everyone feels seen:** the junk drawer, "I'll start Monday" and the all-nighter are universal. The video
  turns the guilt into understanding, the same move that made video 13 (talking to yourself) work.
- **A quotable jaw-dropper:** your brain treats future you like a stranger, and people will happily give future
  them a ketchup-and-soy-sauce drink. "Present you gets the break. Future you gets the ketchup." is the line
  people repeat.
- **A kind, useful payoff:** forgiving yourself makes you procrastinate less. It's counter-intuitive, practical and
  shareable.

**Alternate titles for Test & Compare:**
- Why You Procrastinate (It Has Nothing to Do With Laziness)
- Why Your Brain Treats Future You Like a Stranger
- Procrastinators Aren't Lazy. Here's What's Really Going On

## Tone (locked from video 13)

- **Spoken, not written:** whole, natural sentences, the way you'd explain it to a friend across a table.
- **No "it isn't X, it's Y" formulas** and no strings of two-word fragments.
- **Every line speaks to the viewer** ("you've probably…", "let me ask you something", "if that sounds familiar").

## Engagement design (`GROWTH.md`)

- **Opening: the most relatable image first, then the promise.**
  - **0:00:** "You sit down to finally do the thing you've been putting off. An hour later, you still haven't started it.
    Instead, you went for the easier jobs…": the clean kitchen, the junk drawer, then the octopus video.
  - **About 0:25:** "So in this video, we're going to look at why your brain keeps putting things off…" It states all three
    payoffs, spoken to the viewer.
  - **About 0:40:** the first real finding (Steel 2007: how common it is), then the core idea: it's about feelings
    (Sirois & Pychyl).
- **Two open loops, both paid off:**
  - "Why your brain treats future you like a complete stranger" → paid at about 2:40
  - "The one small habit that can break the cycle" → paid at about 6:00 (self-forgiveness)
- **A re-hook around minute 3:** the ketchup-and-soy-sauce drink.
- **"But it wasn't over yet."** at about 5:55, before the last third.
- **A question roughly every 90 seconds**, plus one sincere question to the viewer: "How many times have you said,
  'I'll start on Monday'?"
- **Irony and human detail:**
  - a clean kitchen and an octopus video instead of the task
  - "if you're watching this instead of doing something else… I won't tell anyone"
  - the relaxed procrastinators who ended up sicker
  - the strangers were famous actors
  - future you gets the ketchup
  - the people who chose their own deadlines knew they needed them, but didn't set them tight enough
  - guilt makes you procrastinate more
  - "at least the kitchen got cleaned"
- **Running image:** the junk drawer opens the video, comes back in the "one habit" section ("think back to the
  start of this video") and closes it.

Numbers and years are written the way they are spoken, so `scripts/14-procrastinate-voiceover.txt` can be pasted
straight into ElevenLabs. The reading guide is `scripts/14-procrastinate-voice-direction.md` ("Pychyl" is
pronounced PITCH-ul, and "Sirois" is pronounced sir-WAH).

| Procrastinators were less stressed and less ill early in the semester; by the end, more stress and illness overall and lower grades | Tice & Baumeister (1997), *Psychological Science* 8(6), 454–458, "Longitudinal study of procrastination, performance, stress, and health" |

**Careful wording kept on purpose:**
- "Their research suggests procrastination is mostly about how a task makes you feel" is a summary of the
  mood-repair view, not a claim that it's the only cause.
- The Hershfield brain finding is "for a lot of people… much like", not "everyone".
- The retirement result was a hypothetical allocation task, so the script says "chose to save".
