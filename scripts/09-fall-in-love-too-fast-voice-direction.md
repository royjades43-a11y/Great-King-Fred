# Voice direction: "The Psychology of People Who Fall in Love Too Fast"

A line-by-line reading guide, in the same format as videos 5 to 8 (see `scripts/05-always-late-voice-direction.md`
for the 12 rules and the full legend). It works for a human narrator, ElevenLabs or AI Studio (use each
section's **Direction** line as the style prompt), and for our free AI voice through `voice/directed.py`.

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **word** | stress it |
| `\|` / `\|\|` | short / long beat |
| ↘ / ↗ | land down / lift (a genuine question) |
| (brisk) (slower) (land it) (lean in) (warm) (smile) (light) (character) | how to deliver the line |

Numbers are written the way they are spoken.

## Energy map

```
energy  5 |
        4 |  *     *  *                                    *
        3 |     *        *   *   *      *        *   *        *
        2 |                        *       *  *                  *
        1 |
          +----------------------------------------------------
            hook bridge  brain  quiz  irony  first-sight numbers rules twist end
```

The hook is playful and knowing. The bridge is a story told with relish. The brain section is curious and a
little awed. The attachment section is warm and gentle (many viewers will recognise themselves). The numbers
are calm and fair. The rules are bright, and the twist is soft and kind.

## 1 · Hook (0:00–0:40)
**Direction:** playful and knowing, like teasing a friend who's "met the one" again. Light on the details
(the dog), a real smile on "Sound familiar?". Energy 4.

(light) Three dates in, | you already **know**. |
(character) This one is **different**. ||
(light) You've pictured the **holidays** together. | (smile) You've maybe even named the **dog**. ||
Your friends tell you to slow **down**. |
(character) But it doesn't feel **fast**. | It feels like **fate**. ||
It happens to **millions** of people. | And for some, | it happens again and **again**. ||
(smile) Sound **familiar**? ↗ ||
Psychologists have studied that rush for **decades**. |
(brisk) With **bridges**, | brain **scanners** | and blood **tests**. ||
(lean in) And what they found | is a little **uncomfortable**. |
The spark you feel | might not be telling you what you **think**. ||
By the end of this video, | you'll know why some people fall so **fast**. |
(lean in) And the **one** question | that tells love apart from something **else**. ||
(smile) Let's start on a very **wobbly** bridge. ||

## 2 · The shaky bridge (0:40–2:05)
**Direction:** a storyteller who loves this one. Paint the bridge (slow on "seventy meters"), act the woman's
line lightly, then punch the numbers. "Half." is a one-word beat. "The bridge did half the flirting" gets a
smile. Energy 4.

(brisk) In nineteen seventy-four, | two psychologists in Canada | ran a famous experiment about **attraction**. |
Their names were Donald **Dutton** | and Arthur **Aron**. ||
Their setting was the Capilano Suspension **Bridge**, | near **Vancouver**. |
It's a narrow walkway of wooden **boards** | and **cables**. |
(slower) It hangs about **seventy meters** | above rocks and **rapids**. |
And it **sways** | when you walk on it. ||
Men crossing the bridge | were stopped by an attractive young **woman**. |
She asked them to fill in a short **questionnaire**. |
Then she tore off a corner of the page | and wrote down her phone **number**. |
(character) "Call me | if you want to talk **more**," | she said. ||
The researchers repeated the experiment on a **second** bridge. |
(light) A **low**, solid one, | just a few meters above a small **stream**. |
Then they **waited** | to see who **called**. ||
From the solid bridge, | only **two** men out of **sixteen** called her. |
(lean in) From the swaying bridge, | it was **nine** out of **eighteen**. ||
(land it) **Half**. ||
(brisk) Same **woman**. | Same **questions**. | Same phone **number**. |
(land it) The only difference | was the **bridge**. ||
And when the interviewer was a **man**, | the bridge made almost no difference at **all**. ||
(lean in) Here's what the researchers think **happened**. |
On the shaky bridge, | the men's hearts were already **pounding**. |
Their bodies were full of **fear**. |
And when an attractive woman appeared, | their brains made a **mistake**. |
(land it) They read the fear | as **attraction**. ||
Psychologists call it | misattribution of **arousal**. |
(smile) In other words, | the bridge did **half** the flirting. ||
So what does a wobbly bridge | have to do with your **love life**? ↗ ||

## 3 · Your brain in love (2:05–3:25)
**Direction:** curious and a little awed, like showing someone an X-ray of their own heart. Clear on the
numbers; a real contrast on "Not calm satisfaction. Wanting." The phone line is a knowing smile. Energy 3.

(land it) Quite a **lot**. |
Because falling in love sets off the same kind of **storm** inside you. ||
In twenty oh five, | a team including Arthur Aron and anthropologist Helen **Fisher** | scanned the brains of **seventeen** people. |
All of them were **intensely** in love. | The longest for seventeen **months**. ||
When they looked at a photo of their partner, | one area lit up most **clearly**. |
A deep part of the brain's **reward** system, | rich in **dopamine**. |
It's linked to **wanting**, | **motivation** | and **craving**. ||
(slower) Not calm **satisfaction**. || (land it) **Wanting**. ||
Another study, in **Italy**, | measured something in people's **blood**. |
**Twenty** people who'd fallen in love in the last six months | were compared with patients with **obsessive-compulsive** disorder. |
(lean in) On one marker of serotonin, | the two groups looked almost the **same**. ||
That might explain | why you can't stop **thinking** about them. |
(smile) It's why new love can make you lose **sleep**, | skip **meals**, | and check your phone again and **again**. ||
When some of the lovers were tested again about a year **later**, | the levels had returned to **normal**. ||
The psychologist Dorothy **Tennov** | had a name for this early, obsessive **stage**. |
(land it) **Limerence**. ||
From her interviews, | she estimated it usually lasts | from about **eighteen months** to **three years**. ||
So that rush is **real**. | (land it) It's also **temporary**. ||
Which raises a **question**. |
(lean in) Why do some people fall into it | so much **faster** than others? ↗ ||

## 4 · The love quiz (3:25–5:20)
**Direction:** warm, gentle, never mocking. Many viewers are the "anxious" group. Read the list of feelings
slowly, with sympathy. The alarm idea is the heart of the section: make it land. The good-news line is
reassuring. The "I love you" fact is a bright, playful surprise. Energy 3, dipping to 2, then up.

In nineteen eighty-seven, | psychologists Cindy **Hazan** and Phillip **Shaver** | printed a "**love quiz**" in a local newspaper. |
Six hundred and twenty people | sent in their **answers**. ||
About **fifty-six** percent | described a **secure** style of love. |
(warm) Trusting, | **comfortable**, | steady. ||
About **twenty-five** percent were **avoidant**. | They kept partners at a **distance**. ||
And about **nineteen** percent | were what they called **anxious**. ||
Those people described love very **differently**. |
(slower) As **obsession**. || Emotional **highs** and **lows**. || Extreme **attraction**. ||
A desperate wish | for the feeling to be **returned**. || And **jealousy**. ||
For anxious people, | love doesn't arrive **slowly**. |
(land it) It hits like a **wave**. ||
Psychiatrist Amir **Levine** and psychologist Rachel **Heller** | go one step **further**. |
(lean in) They argue that anxious people often mistake an **alarm** | for **chemistry**. |
When someone is **hot** and **cold**, | the uncertainty keeps your heart **racing**. |
And that racing | feels like **passion**. ||
(lean in) Remember the **bridge**? ↗ ||
(land it) Sometimes the bridge | is the **person**. ||
(smile) Here's the **irony**. |
A calm, kind partner | can feel **boring** at first. |
Not because something is **missing**. |
(land it) Because nothing is setting off the **alarm**. ||
(warm) Have you ever mistaken a pounding heart | for a perfect **match**? ↗ ||
(warm) The good news | is that these styles aren't **fixed**. |
Many people become more **secure** over time, | often with a **steady** partner. ||
(brisk) And here's one more **surprise**. |
Most people believe **women** fall in love first. |
In twenty eleven, | a team led by Joshua **Ackerman** | found the **opposite**. |
**Men** thought about saying "I love you" | about **six weeks** earlier than women. |
(smile) And in most couples, | the **man** said it first. ||

## 5 · Love at first sight (5:20–6:05)
**Direction:** a gentle myth-buster, never cynical. "Not one case… was mutual" is a quiet, slightly funny
beat. "Chemistry is real" is said warmly, then the paper joke lands light. The cliffhanger leans in.
Energy 3.

(brisk) So what about love at first **sight**? ↗ ||
In twenty seventeen, | a team in the **Netherlands** | studied people who said they'd **felt** it. |
They found it wasn't marked by **intimacy**, | **passion** | or **commitment**. |
Its strongest link | was physical **attraction**. ||
(smile) At their speed-dating events, | not **one** case of love at first sight | was **mutual**. ||
And many people only decided it had been love at first sight | **later**, | looking **back**. ||
That same year, | researchers tried to predict attraction | with a **computer**. |
Before speed dating, | people answered more than a **hundred** questions about themselves. |
(land it) The computer still couldn't predict | who would want **whom**. ||
(warm) Chemistry is **real**. | But you can't find it on **paper**. |
(smile) And three dates | is not much more than **paper**. ||
(lean in) But it wasn't over **yet**. ||

## 6 · What the numbers say (6:05–6:55)
**Direction:** calm, fair and clear, like a good teacher with a chart. Be honest on the caveat (it's
important). Slow down on "see who's actually there". Energy 2–3.

(slower) Because there's a **cost** to falling too fast, | and it shows up in the **numbers**. ||
In twenty fifteen, | economists Andrew Francis-Tan and Hugo **Mialon** | surveyed about **three thousand** people who had been married. |
Couples who dated for **one to two** years before getting engaged | did **better**. |
They were about **twenty percent** less likely to divorce | than couples who dated for under a **year**. |
(lean in) Couples who dated for **three** years or more | were about **thirty-nine percent** less likely. ||
(warm) That doesn't **prove** | that waiting causes a better marriage. |
Couples who wait | may be different in other **ways**. ||
But time does **one** thing | nothing else can. |
(slower) It lets the rush wear **off**, | so you can see who's **actually** there. ||
Remember Tennov's **estimate**. | **Eighteen months** to **three years**. |
That's roughly how long the rush **lasts**, | and roughly how long the **safer** couples waited. ||
(brisk) So how do you fall in love | at the **right** speed? ↗ ||

## 7 · Five rules (6:55–7:50)
**Direction:** a bright, practical coach. Each "Rule…" is a clean new step with a little smile. The
one-word list in rule two is quick and funny ("Wrong." gets a beat). Energy 4.

(brisk) Rule **one**: | do the **bridge** check. |
When your heart is racing, | ask **one** question. |
(character) Is it **them**, | or is it the **uncertainty**? ↗ |
(warm) If you feel **calm** and **safe**, | that's a **good** sign, | not a boring one. ||

(brisk) Rule **two**: | see them in every **weather**. |
(light) **Tired**. | **Stressed**. | **Hungry**. || (smile) **Wrong**. ||
You don't really know someone | until you've seen them on a **bad** day. ||

(brisk) Rule **three**: | keep your **own** life. |
Your **friends**, | your **hobbies**, | your **routines**. |
Falling fast often means | letting everything else fall **away**. ||

(brisk) Rule **four**: | let **actions** catch up with **feelings**. |
(light) Words are **fast**. | Consistency is **slow**. |
Watch what someone **does**, | week after **week**. ||

(brisk) And rule **five**: | give it **time**. |
The rush can last a **year** or more. |
Let some of it **settle** | before you make **big** decisions. ||

## 8 · The twist and the ending (7:50–8:30)
**Direction:** the softest part. Kind, never preachy: we are on the side of people who fall fast. "Make sure
it's them, not the bridge." is the last line that matters: slow, warm, landed. The challenge and CTA are
friendly and light. Energy 2, then 4.

(slower) So here's the **twist**. ||
Falling fast | isn't a **flaw**. |
(warm) It often comes from a **big** heart, | and a brain doing **exactly** what it evolved to do. |
(smile) People who fall fast | love with their **whole chest**. | That's worth **keeping**. |
Just add a little **patience**. ||
The problem isn't how fast you **fall**. |
(land it) It's how fast you **decide**. ||
(warm) The men on that bridge | felt something **real**. |
They just didn't know | where it was coming **from**. ||
(slower) So the next time your heart is **pounding**, | take one more step | before you **jump**. |
(land it) Make sure it's **them**, | not the **bridge**. ||

(brisk) So here's your **challenge** for this week. |
Think about the last time | you fell for someone **fast**. |
Ask yourself **honestly**: | (character) was it **them**, | or the **bridge**? ↗ |
Then come back and tell me what you **found**. ||

(smile) Are you someone who falls **fast**, | or someone who takes their **time**? ↗ | Tell me in the comments. |
Which habit should we cover **next**? ↗ | Drop it in the comments. ↘
