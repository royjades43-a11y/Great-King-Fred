# Voice direction: "The Psychology of People Who Never Ask for Help"

A line-by-line reading guide, in the same format as video 5's (see `scripts/05-always-late-voice-direction.md`
for the 12 rules and the full legend). It works for a human narrator, ElevenLabs or AI Studio (use each
section's **Direction** line as the style prompt), and for our free AI voice through `voice/directed.py`.

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **word** | stress it |
| `\|` / `\|\|` | short / long beat |
| ↘ / ↗ | land down / lift (a genuine question) |
| (brisk) (slower) (land it) (lean in) (warm) (smile) (light) (character) | how to deliver the line |

Numbers are written the way they are spoken.

## Energy map

```
energy  5 |  *
        4 |  *        *     *                         *
        3 |     *  *     *     *  *  *      *     *       *
        2 |                             *  *
        1 |
          +----------------------------------------------------
            hook Franklin study  pickle fears  cost  rules twist end
```

The hook is the most playful (we're teasing the viewer), the cost section is the quietest, the rules are
bright and coach-like, and the twist lands warm and slow.

---

## 1 · Hook (0:00–0:35)
**Direction:** playful, knowing, like catching a friend doing the thing. Light and quick, with a real
smile on "Sound familiar?". Energy 4–5.

(lean in) You're **lost**. ||
There's a person **right there**. | You could just **ask**. ||
(light) Instead, you walk around the same block | **three** times. ||
(character) Or you're buried at work, someone asks if you're okay, | and you say, | "I'm **fine**." ||
(smile) Sound **familiar**? ↗ ||
(lean in) Here's the **strange** part. |
Psychologists have tested this, | with **thousands** of strangers. |
And the people who won't ask | are wrong about almost **everything**. ||
(brisk) Wrong about how many people will say **yes**. | Wrong about how they'll **feel**. | Wrong about how they'll **see** you. ||
By the end of this video, you'll know **why**. |
And the **one thing** that makes people say yes. | (lean in) It isn't what you'd **guess**. ||
(brisk) Let's start with a man who figured this out | almost **three hundred** years ago. ||

## 2 · The rival and the book (0:35–1:50)
**Direction:** a storyteller by the fire. Enjoy the cleverness of Franklin's move. Pace: conversational,
a beat before the reveal ("borrow it"). Energy 3–4.

It's the story of one of history's most famous people, | and one of his **enemies**. |
In the seventeen-thirties, Benjamin **Franklin** had a problem in the Pennsylvania Assembly. |
He'd just been chosen as its **clerk**, | and he wanted to keep the job. |
A new member, wealthy and well-educated, | made a long speech **against** him. ||
(light) Franklin didn't want to **grovel**. | He didn't want to **fight**. |
(lean in) So he did something **strange**. ||
He heard that this rival owned a **rare**, unusual book. |
And he wrote him a note, | asking if he could **borrow** it. ||
(brisk) The rival sent it **straight away**. |
Franklin returned it about a week later, | with a note of **thanks**. ||
(warm) The next time they met, the man **spoke** to him, | for the first time, | and with real **courtesy**. |
They became **friends**, | and stayed friends until the man's death. ||
Franklin wrote down the lesson in his autobiography. |
He called it an old saying he'd learned, | now proven **true**. ||
(character) "He that has once done you a **kindness** | will be more ready to do you **another**, | than he whom you yourself have obliged." ||
(land it) Asking didn't **cost** him anything. | It won him a **friend**. ||
(slower) So why do so many of us feel the exact **opposite**? ||

## 3 · The experiments (1:50–3:20)
**Direction:** curious and building. Set up each number as a guess-versus-reality reveal; the "Half."
is a one-word punch. Energy 3, rising to 4 on "pickle".

In nineteen sixty-nine, two psychologists **tested** Franklin's idea. |
People took part in a contest | and won some **money**. |
Afterwards, the researcher asked some of them for a **favor**. |
(character) Could they give the money **back**? ↗ | He'd been paying out of his own **pocket**. ||
The people he asked | ended up liking him **more** | than the people he didn't. |
(warm) A favor didn't push them **away**. | It pulled them **closer**. ||
(brisk) Then, in two thousand eight, researchers at Stanford asked a simpler question. |
(character) How many strangers do you think you'd need to ask, | before one says **yes**? ↗ ||
In one task, students had to get a stranger | to walk them to the campus **gym**. |
They guessed they'd need to ask about | **seven** people. ||
(land it) In reality, | it took just over **two**. ||
In another study, | volunteers raised money for **charity**. |
They expected to approach about | **two hundred and ten** people to hit their goal. |
(slower) They needed about | **one hundred and twenty-two**. ||
And across studies with more than **fourteen thousand** strangers, | people underestimated how often they'd hear yes | by about **half**. ||
(land it) **Half**. ||
We think the world is full of people who'll say **no**. |
(warm) It's mostly full of people who'll say **yes**. ||
(lean in) And it goes further than you'd **think**. |
In one study, people asked strangers to do something | a little bit **wrong**. |
(light) Write the word | "**pickle**," | in pen, | in a library book. ||
(smile) Even then, | **more** people agreed than the askers expected. ||
(light) If strangers will deface a library book for you, | they'll probably hold a **door**. ||
(slower) So why are we so sure they **won't**? ||
Because when you're about to ask, | your brain makes **three** predictions. |
(land it) And all three | are **wrong**. ||

## 4 · Three fears (3:20–5:35)
**Direction:** a clear numbered list. Each "Fear number…" is a fresh start, slightly brighter. The
research lines are calm and confident; "Not less. More." is a punch. Energy 3–4.

(warm) Many of us learned early | that needing help means you didn't try hard **enough**. |
So we carry **three** fears into every ask. ||
(brisk) Fear number **one**: | (character) "They'll say **no**." ||
Here's what your brain **forgets**. |
When you picture asking, | you picture **your** embarrassment. |
(lean in) You don't picture **theirs**. ||
Saying no to a real person, face to face, | is awkward **too**. |
Askers focus on how hard it is to **ask**. |
They forget how hard it is to **refuse**. |
(smile) That's why the yeses keep catching them off **guard**. ||

(brisk) Fear number **two**: | (character) "I'll be a **burden**." ||
In twenty twenty-two, psychologists ran a set of experiments | with more than **two thousand** people. |
In one, people asked strangers in a park | to take their **photo**. |
The askers expected the helpers to feel **inconvenienced**. ||
(land it) They were **wrong**. ||
The helpers were more **willing**, | felt **better** afterwards, | and felt far less put out than the askers predicted. |
The team also asked people to remember times they'd helped someone **else**. |
(warm) Most of those memories were **warm** ones. | Being asked had felt **good**. ||
We think we're **taking** something from people. |
(warm) Often, we're **giving** them something. | A small chance to be **kind**. ||

(brisk) Fear number **three**: | (character) "They'll think I'm **weak**." ||
(lean in) This is the **big** one. |
In twenty fifteen, researchers at Harvard and Wharton tested it **directly**. |
In one study, about a hundred and seventy people solved **brainteasers** with a partner. |
Some partners asked for their **advice**. | Some **didn't**. ||
The people who were asked for advice | rated their partner as more **competent**. ||
(land it) Not **less**. | **More**. ||
Especially when the task was **hard**. |
And on easy tasks, | asking didn't hurt at **all**. ||
(smile) Being asked makes people feel **smart**. | And they like the person who made them feel that way. ||
There was one **catch**. |
It worked best when the person was asked **directly**, | and when they actually knew the **topic**. |
(slower) So the fear of looking weak | has it **backwards**. ||
(warm) Think about the last time someone asked for **your** advice. |
Did you think **less** of them? ↗ | Probably not. | (smile) You probably felt a little **flattered**. ||
(brisk) So which fear is **yours**? ↗ | Number one, | two, | or three? | Put it in the comments. ||

## 5 · What it costs (5:35–6:20)
**Direction:** the quiet, honest middle. Slower, lower, no judgement; a list of small sad pictures.
Epley's line is said plainly, then a beat. Energy 2.

(slower) Now think about what never asking | really **costs**. ||
The project that takes **three** days | instead of **one**. |
The problem that **grows** while you wait. |
The question you never ask in class, | so you stay **confused** for the rest of the term. |
(warm) The friend who would have helped in **ten minutes**, | and never knew you **needed** it. ||
(slower) And the slow, lonely feeling | of carrying **everything** by yourself. ||
Psychologist Nicholas Epley, who worked on that twenty twenty-two study, | put it **bluntly**. |
Not asking, when someone would be happy to help, | isn't **kind**. ||
(land it) It's a little **unkind**. ||
Because you're taking away **their** chance to help. ||
(slower) It sounds **harsh**. ||
(lean in) But it wasn't over **yet**. ||

## 6 · How to ask (6:20–7:35)
**Direction:** bright, practical, coach-like. Each rule is a clean new step. The promised "one thing"
gets slowed right down. Energy 4.

(brisk) So what actually **works**? ↗ | How do you **ask**? ↗ ||
(brisk) Rule **one**: | ask in **person**. |
Not a **text**. | Not an **email**. | Walk over, | or at least **call**. ||
In twenty seventeen, researchers had people ask strangers | to fill in a short **survey**. |
Some asked by **email**. | Some asked **face to face**. |
Both groups expected about the **same** success. ||
(slower) But the face-to-face requests | were about **thirty-four times** more effective. ||
(lean in) And here's the twist inside the **twist**. |
When people ask by email, | they do the **opposite** of everything we've seen. |
They **overestimate** the yeses. |
Later work found video and phone calls | land somewhere in **between**. ||
(slower) That's the thing I **promised** you. ||
(land it) It isn't the perfect **wording**. | It's **showing up**. ||
An email is easy to **ignore**. | A person standing in front of you | is **not**. ||
(brisk) Rule **two**: | be **specific**. |
(character) "Could you look at this for **ten minutes** on **Friday**?" ↗ |
That's easy to say **yes** to. |
(character) "Can you help me **sometime**?" ↗ |
That's easy to put **off**. ||
(brisk) Rule **three**: | ask for **advice**, | not just help. |
(character) "What would **you** do?" | makes the other person the **expert**. |
And as we just saw, | that makes them think **more** of you, | not less. ||
(brisk) Rule **four**: | make "no" **easy**. |
Remember, | saying no face to face is **hard**. | So give people a real way **out**. |
(warm) "No worries if you can't" | turns pressure into an **invitation**. |
The people who do say yes | will **mean** it. ||

## 7 · The twist and the ending (7:35–8:15)
**Direction:** warm, slow and sure, then the challenge is friendly and upbeat. Energy 2, then 4 for the
challenge and the CTA.

(slower) So here's the **twist**. ||
People who never ask for help | think they're **protecting** themselves. |
From the **no**. | From being a **burden**. | From looking **weak**. ||
But the research says the no | is **rarer** than you think. |
The burden is **lighter** than you think. |
(land it) And the weakness is in **your** head, | not **theirs**. ||
(warm) One of the most impressive people of his century | didn't win over his rival by being **impressive**. |
He won him over | by asking to borrow a **book**. ||
(land it) Sometimes the strongest thing you can do | is let someone **help** you. ||
(smile) And the next time you're lost, | just **ask**. |
Odds are, | the **second** person you stop | will walk you there. ||

(brisk) So here's your **challenge** for this week. |
Ask **one** person | for **one** small, specific thing. | In **person**. |
Then come back and tell me what **happened**. ||

(smile) Are you the one who never **asks**, | or the one who always **helps**? ↗ | Tell me in the comments. |
Which habit should we cover **next**? ↗ | Drop it in the comments. ↘
