## Thumbnails (locked from video 12, Nov 2026)

The layout follows big psychology channels like KnowSense, but in our own brand.

- **One big character on the right**, drawn as a clean ink illustration (not a stick figure, not AI art, never a
  real or famous person). It should explain the feeling in one glance and make people curious. It can be a
  person, or an object or creature people instantly relate to:
  - a skeleton still waiting on an "ok." text
  - a ghost whispering "what if…?"
  - a puppet on red strings saying "Sure!"
  - a heart on a fishhook, stitched up from last time
  - a heart skydiving with its parachute still packed
  - a sweating phone with a thumb over Send
  - a head with a tangled scribble inside
  - two skeletons holding hands
- **Short text on the left**: Montserrat 800, sentence case, black, 2–5 words, 140–165 px on the 1920×1080 canvas.
- **Exactly one key word in a red box** with white letters (`.box`), sometimes two short words.
- **One small speech bubble at most** (card fill, thick black border), only when it adds the joke: "ok.", "Sure!", "what if…?".
- **No** logos, channel name, borders, arrows or emojis.
- **Three per video:** A is the main one, plus B and C for Test & Compare. Each tests a different reason to click,
  not a recolour.
- **Checks before export:**
  - the one-second test at phone size: can you tell what the video is about?
  - nothing overlaps the text
  - the character fills about half the frame
- **Source:**
  - `<project>/thumbnail/figures.mjs` holds the drawings; reuse and extend it.
  - `make.mjs` lays them out.
  - Snapshot with HyperFrames and export 1280×720 JPGs as `videos/NN-thumbnail-X-name.jpg`.

## Videos

- **Look:** stick figures and line icons on the sage stage. No chapter cards, no double captions. One text zone at
  the top with short phrases (one word in red), timed to the voice.
- **Length:** over 8:00 (about 9:00), 4K master under 100 MB (CRF 19), mixed at -14 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP.
- **Script rules:**
  - Talk straight to the viewer.
  - No sentence over 20 words, and no filler openers.
  - No "amazingly", "incredibly" or "surprisingly".
  - A rhetorical question about every 90 seconds, and at least 3 irony or human-detail moments.
  - Put "But it wasn't over yet." before the last third.
  - Use real studies only: author and year, careful wording, sources in the description.
  - Always end with "Which habit should we cover next? Drop it in the comments."
  - **Write it to be spoken (owner's rule, Nov 2026).** Use whole, natural sentences, like a friend telling a story.
    - Avoid "it isn't X, it's Y" formulas ("It isn't babbling. It's thinking.") and strings of clipped fragments.
      They sound AI-written.
    - Make every line speak to the viewer ("you've probably…", "let me ask you…", "if you're like most people"),
      so they feel part of the video.
  - **Open with the most relatable image, then say what the video is about** within about 20 seconds ("So in this
    video, we're going to look at… By the end, you'll know…").
  - **Tell the research as a story, not a citation (owner's rule, Nov 2026).**
    - No years and no researcher names in the voiceover. Not "In 1997, psychologists Tice and Baumeister…", but
      "Picture a college class in the first week of term. Two researchers decided to follow every student…"
    - Say "researchers", "a team of psychologists", "scientists at a university" or "one study".
    - Open each study as a scene ("Picture this…", "Imagine you signed up for…", "Here's what one team tried…"), so
      the viewer walks through the experiment.
    - Callbacks use the story, not the name: "the same team behind the button study".
    - Findings stay accurate and carefully worded ("most people", "on average", "later studies don't all agree").
    - Names, years and journals go only in the description's source list. On-screen study cards carry a short
      story title ("The semester study"), with no names or years.
  - **Copyright-safe:**
    - Describe findings in our own words, and never read out or show passages from papers.
    - Use only our own drawings, our own music and Pixabay sound effects.
    - Avoid real famous people's names and likenesses unless the story truly needs them; prefer "a famous athlete…".
- **Voiceover:** ElevenLabs (Josh), generated by the owner from the numbers-spelled-out text. Verified with Whisper.
  Used as is, with no pauses cut.

## Performance and art direction (from video 13, Nov 2026)

- **A host:** one stick figure performs the narration silently, like a mime (owner's rule, Nov 2026).
  - **No talking:** mouths never move with the voice. There's no lip-sync (`talk`) on the host or on story figures.
  - **Gestures carry the line:** explain, present, chin, ear, lean, count, embarrassed, shrug and so on.
  - **Faces only when the moment calls for it:** a smile, a grin, surprise (`o`), worry (`frown`, `tight`). The
    rest of the time the face is neutral or a soft smile.
  - It reacts to the story figures, then turns back to the viewer.
- **Story figures act out the studies and moments.** No floating icons where a person could act it instead.
- **Every chapter happens somewhere:** a soft, muted line-drawn environment (playroom, kitchen, study, court,
  stage…).
- **Calm and warm, nothing dramatic.** Emotion comes from posture and expression, not big motion.
- **Callbacks:** the opening image returns at the end.

## Shorts (locked Nov 2026: original only)

- **Never cut from a long video.** Every Short has its own script, voiceover and visuals, built natively at
  1080×1920.
- **One idea per Short:** one finding, one twist, one takeaway. Most stand alone. Some are made to "trail" a long
  video on the same theme, so they can link to it.
- **Length:** 20–45 s, which is 60–125 words at our voice's pace.
- **Script shape:**
  1. **Hook (first line, under 2 s):** a "you" statement or question that is only half answered, such as "Your
     future best friend might live next door." The same words go on screen as the opening phrase, held for the
     first 2 s so the feed preview shows them.
  2. **Proof (one study):** who, when and what they found, in plain words, with one concrete detail people
     remember (the pigeon, the pickle, the T-shirt).
  3. **Turn:** the irony or the "so that's why" moment.
  4. **Takeaway:** one line the viewer can use today.
  5. **Last line:** a comment question, written so it leads back into the hook when the Short loops.
- **Script rules:** the same as long videos (sentences of 20 words or fewer, no filler openers, real sources,
  numbers spelled out for ElevenLabs), but no "But it wasn't over yet." and no fixed sign-off.
- **Visuals:**
  - our sage, ink and red look with Montserrat
  - big stick figures centred in the safe zone
  - one drawing or motion per line
  - a short phrase at the top that changes with each beat
  - word-by-word captions in the lower middle
  - nothing in the bottom 20% or the right-hand 12% (where YouTube's buttons sit)
- **Covers:** the locked thumbnail style, recomposed to vertical: character in the middle, 2–4 words above it,
  one word in a red box.
- **Upload copy:**
  - a title of 50 characters or fewer, with the hook, and no question mark at the end
  - a 2-line description with the source, plus #shorts and 2 topic tags
  - a pinned A-or-B comment question
  - a Related Video link to the closest long video
- **Files:**
  - scripts in `scripts/shorts-original-NN-NN.md` (a Voiceover block to paste, visual beats and source)
  - renders in `videos/shorts-original/`
