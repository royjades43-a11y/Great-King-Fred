## Thumbnails (locked from video 12, Nov 2026)

The layout follows big psychology channels like KnowSense, but in our own brand.

- **One big character on the right**, drawn as a clean ink illustration (not a stick figure, not AI art, never a
  real or famous person). It should explain the feeling in one glance and make people curious. It can be a
  person, or an object or creature people instantly relate to:
  - a skeleton still waiting on an "ok." text
  - a ghost whispering "what if…?"
  - a puppet on red strings saying "Sure!"
  - a heart on a fishhook, stitched up from last time
  - a heart skydiving with its parachute still packed
  - a sweating phone with a thumb over Send
  - a head with a tangled scribble inside
  - two skeletons holding hands
- **Short text on the left**: Montserrat 800, sentence case, black, 2–5 words, 140–165 px on the 1920×1080 canvas.
- **Exactly one key word in a red box** with white letters (`.box`), sometimes two short words.
- **One small speech bubble at most** (card fill, thick black border), only when it adds the joke: "ok.", "Sure!", "what if…?".
- **No** logos, channel name, borders, arrows or emojis.
- **Three per video:** A is the main one, plus B and C for Test & Compare. Each tests a different reason to click,
  not a recolour.
- **Checks before export:**
  - the one-second test at phone size: can you tell what the video is about?
  - nothing overlaps the text
  - the character fills about half the frame
- **Source:**
  - `<project>/thumbnail/figures.mjs` holds the drawings; reuse and extend it.
  - `make.mjs` lays them out.
  - Snapshot with HyperFrames and export 1280×720 JPGs as `videos/NN-thumbnail-X-name.jpg`.

## Videos

- **Look:** stick figures and line icons on the sage stage. No chapter cards, no double captions. One text zone at
  the top with short phrases (one word in red), timed to the voice.
- **Length:** over 8:00 (about 9:00), 4K master under 100 MB (CRF 19), mixed at -14 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP.
- **Script rules:**
  - Talk straight to the viewer.
  - No sentence over 20 words, and no filler openers.
  - No "amazingly", "incredibly" or "surprisingly".
  - A rhetorical question about every 90 seconds, and at least 3 irony or human-detail moments.
  - Put "But it wasn't over yet." before the last third.
  - Use real studies only: author and year, careful wording, sources in the description.
  - Always end with "Which habit should we cover next? Drop it in the comments."
- **Voiceover:** ElevenLabs (Josh), generated by the owner from the numbers-spelled-out text. Verified with Whisper.
  Used as is, with no pauses cut.

## Titles and descriptions
