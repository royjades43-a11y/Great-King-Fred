# Voice direction: "The Psychology of People Who Overthink Everything"

A line-by-line reading guide, in the same format as videos 5 to 11 (see `scripts/05-always-late-voice-direction.md`
for the 12 rules and the full legend). It works for a human narrator, ElevenLabs or AI Studio (use each
section's **Direction** line as the style prompt), and for our free AI voice through `voice/directed.py`.

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **word** | stress it |
| `\|` / `\|\|` | short / long beat |
| ↘ / ↗ | land down / lift (a genuine question) |
| (brisk) (slower) (land it) (lean in) (warm) (smile) (light) (character) | how to deliver the line |

Numbers are written the way they are spoken.

## Energy map

```
energy  5 |
        4 |  *           *  *              *        *  *
        3 |     *  *  *        *     *  *     *  *        *
        2 |  *                    *                          *  *
        1 |
          +----------------------------------------------------------
            hook   wandering  spotlight  loops  decisions  plan  rules  twist end
```

The hook is late-night and a little funny: one person, one "ok." text, forty minutes of worry. The wandering-mind
section is curious. The spotlight section is the fun one: play the Barry Manilow shirt with a smile. The rumination
section is honest and calm, then relieved at "about ninety-one percent hadn't". Decisions are wry ("they did
better, and felt worse"). The plan section is the payoff, bright and practical. The rules are friendly, and the
twist is warm and generous.

This script talks straight to the viewer ("sound familiar?", "that conversation you've been replaying",
"how much of your week"). Read those asides as if to one friend, a little closer than the lines around them.

---

## 1 · Hook (0:00–0:50)
**Direction:** late at night, quiet and a little amused. Read "ok." flat, exactly like the text looks. The
three questions speed up, the way a spiral does. "And they're asleep." is a dry punchline. Energy 2, rising
to 4 by the promise.

(slower) It's **late**. | You should be **asleep**. | Instead, you're staring at **one** text. ||
(character) You wrote, | "Sounds **good**! | Can't **wait**!" |
They wrote back, | (flat) "**ok**." ||
No capital letter. | No exclamation mark. | Just | "**ok**." ||
(brisk) Are they **mad**? ↗ | Did you say something **wrong**? ↗ | Was the exclamation mark **too much**? ↗ ||
(slower) Forty minutes later, | you're still **thinking** about it. ||
(smile) And they're **asleep**. ||
(warm) Sound **familiar**? ↗ ||
You're not **crazy**, | and you're not **dramatic**. |
(lean in) Your brain thinks it's doing its **job**. |
It's trying to keep you safe | by thinking it through **one more time**. |
(smile) And then one more time after **that**. ||
(brisk) Psychologists have studied what all that thinking actually **does**. |
They've pinged thousands of people to ask what's on their **minds**. |
They've made students wear an embarrassing **T-shirt**. |
And they've checked how many worries ever **come true**. ||
By the end of this video, | you'll know why your mind keeps **replaying** things. |
(lean in) And the **one** simple move | that helps it **let go**. ||
Let's start with where your mind goes | when you're not **looking**. ||

## 2 · The wandering mind (0:50–1:55)
**Direction:** curious, like a story you just read. The three app questions are quick and plain. "Forty-six
point nine percent" gets a tiny smile. The paper's title is quoted slowly. Energy 3.

In two thousand ten, | Harvard psychologists Matthew Killingsworth and Daniel **Gilbert** built an iPhone **app**. |
It pinged people at **random** moments during their day. |
It asked three **things**. |
(brisk) What are you **doing**? ↘ | What are you **thinking** about? ↘ | How do you **feel**? ↘ ||
More than two **thousand** people answered. |
Their minds were somewhere **else** | almost **half** the time. |
(smile) Forty-six point nine percent, | to be **exact**. ||
In the **shower**. | At **work**. | Even in the middle of a **conversation**. ||
And when their minds wandered, | people were less **happy**. |
Even pleasant daydreams didn't make them any happier | than staying **present**. |
(slower) The researchers put it right in their title. |
"A wandering mind | is an **unhappy** mind." ||
(lean in) For an overthinker, | the mind doesn't just **wander**. |
It wanders back to the **same spot**. |
The **text**. | The **meeting**. | (smile) The thing you said at a party | six **years** ago. ||
So what's it **looking** for back there? ↗ ||

## 3 · The spotlight (1:55–3:15)
**Direction:** the fun one. "Slightly cruel experiment" with a grin, "Barry Manilow" played straight. The
spotlight line lands, then the "on stage too" line is a gentle laugh. The liking gap is warm and reassuring.
Energy 4.

(lean in) Usually, | it's looking for **one** thing. | What other people think of **you**. |
And here's the **problem**. |
You think they're thinking about you | much **more** than they are. ||
In two thousand, | psychologist Thomas **Gilovich** and his colleagues | ran a slightly **cruel** experiment. |
They had college students put on a T-shirt | with Barry **Manilow's** face on it. |
(smile) For those students, | that was **deeply** embarrassing. |
Then each student walked into a room | full of their **peers**. ||
Afterward, | the wearers guessed that about **half** the room had noticed the shirt. |
(land it) In reality, | only about a **quarter** had. ||
Psychologists call this the **spotlight** effect. |
You feel like you're on **stage**. |
(smile) Everyone else is busy | feeling like **they're** on stage too. ||
And it doesn't stop at **T-shirts**. |
In two thousand eighteen, | psychologist Erica **Boothby** and her colleagues | paired up **strangers** for a chat. |
Afterward, | each person guessed how much the other one **liked** them. |
Most people guessed too **low**. |
(warm) Their partners liked them **more**, | and enjoyed the talk more, | than they **realized**. |
The researchers called it the **liking gap**. |
Between new college roommates, | it lasted for **months**. ||
(lean in) So that conversation you've been replaying all **week**? ↗ |
(warm) The other person probably walked away | thinking it went **fine**. ||

## 4 · Loops (3:15–4:40)
**Direction:** honest and calm. "Is it?" is a real question. "It was actually a loop." lands down. The worry
study builds, then relief on "hadn't". The closing question is personal. Energy 3.

But overthinking feels **useful**. |
It feels like you're **working** on the problem. |
(lean in) **Is** it? ↗ ||
Psychologists have a word for this. | **Rumination**. |
(smile) It comes from the way a cow chews its **cud**. |
Chew, | swallow, | bring it **back** up, | chew it **again**. ||
In nineteen ninety-five, | psychologists Sonja Lyubomirsky and Susan Nolen-**Hoeksema** tested whether it **helps**. |
They brought in people who were already feeling **down**. |
Some spent a few minutes **focusing** on their feelings, | and why they felt that **way**. |
Others spent those minutes thinking about something **else** entirely. |
Then everyone tried to solve everyday problems | with other **people**. |
(land it) The ruminators came up with **worse** solutions. |
They felt **worse**, too. ||
(slower) The thinking felt like **progress**. |
It was actually a **loop**. ↘ ||
And the things you're **worried** about? ↗ |
In twenty twenty, | psychologists Lucas LaFreniere and Michelle **Newman** ran a simple **test**. |
People with anxiety wrote down their worries for **ten** days. |
Later, | they checked which ones had actually come **true**. |
(warm) About **ninety-one** percent | **hadn't**. ||
For many people, | not a **single** worry came true. ||
Each worry felt like an **emergency**. |
(land it) Most were **false alarms**. ||
(lean in) So how much of your **week** | have you spent on something that never **happened**? ↗ ||

## 5 · Decisions (4:40–5:55)
**Direction:** wry. The poster study is a little story. "They did better, and felt worse." is the punchline,
slowly. Then a real pause before "But it wasn't over yet.", which is low and close. Energy 3, rising to 4.

Overthinking hits **decisions** hardest. |
And here, | more thinking can lead to a **worse** choice. ||
In nineteen ninety-three, | psychologist Timothy **Wilson** and his colleagues | let students pick a free **poster** to take home. |
Some just picked the one they **liked**. |
Others first wrote down **why** they liked or disliked each one. |
**Weeks** later, | the researchers called them **back**. |
(land it) The students who had analyzed their choice | were **less** happy with their poster. |
Writing reasons pushed them toward what was easy to **explain**, | not what they really **liked**. ||
In two thousand six, | psychologists Sheena Iyengar, Rachael Wells, and Barry **Schwartz** | followed students looking for their first **jobs**. |
Some were **maximizers**. |
They wanted the **best** possible option, | not just a **good** one. |
The maximizers landed jobs paying about **twenty** percent more. |
(slower) And yet, | they felt **worse**. |
They were less **satisfied** with the job they chose. |
They felt more stressed and **anxious** along the way. |
(smile) They did **better**, | and felt **worse**. ||
Schwartz had written a whole book about this, | called The Paradox of **Choice**. |
More options, | he argued, | can leave us **less** satisfied. |
(lean in) Ever spent forty minutes picking a **show**, | and then watched **nothing**? ↗ ||
(slower) (lean in) But it wasn't over **yet**. ||

## 6 · The plan (5:55–7:15)
**Direction:** the payoff of the hook's promise, bright and clear. "They didn't do the task. They just planned
it." is the key moment, slower. The name trick is playful, then sincere. The closing question is warm.
Energy 4.

Because there's a **reason** your brain won't drop a thought. |
(land it) **Unfinished** business sticks. |
Your mind keeps tapping you on the **shoulder** | about what isn't **done**. ||
It's often called the **Zeigarnik** effect, | after psychologist Bluma **Zeigarnik**. |
(light) As the story goes, | it started in a **restaurant** in the nineteen twenties. |
The waiters remembered every **unpaid** order. |
Once the bill was **paid**, | the order vanished from their **heads**. |
Her experiments found that people recalled **interrupted** tasks | better than **finished** ones. ||
In two thousand eleven, | psychologists E. J. Masicampo and Roy **Baumeister** | tested how to make it **stop**. |
They had people think about an important goal | they hadn't **finished** yet. |
Then they gave them a passage to **read**. |
Their minds kept drifting **back** to the goal. ||
Then some people did **one** small thing. |
They wrote a **specific** plan for it. |
When, | **where**, | and **how**. ||
(slower) They didn't **do** the task. | They just **planned** it. |
And the intrusive thoughts | **largely** went away. ||
(warm) Your brain doesn't need the problem solved **tonight**. |
It needs to know there's a **plan**. ||
(light) There's one more trick, | and it sounds a little **silly**. |
In two thousand fourteen, | psychologist Ethan **Kross** and his colleagues | gave people five minutes to prepare a **speech**. |
Some talked to themselves as | "**I**." |
Others used their own **name**. |
(character) "Sam, | you can **do** this." ||
The name group felt less **anxious**. |
Judges rated their speeches **better**. |
And afterward, | they spent less time beating themselves **up**. ||
Using your name puts a little **distance** between you and the problem. |
It's like giving advice to a **friend**. ||
(lean in) Why is it so much easier | to fix a friend's problem than your **own**? ↗ ||

## 7 · Five rules (7:15–8:20)
**Direction:** friendly and practical, like a coach who likes you. Each "Rule" line is a small headline. The
example lines are spoken naturally. Energy 3.

(brisk) So here's what you can **do** with all this. ||
Rule **one**: | turn the worry into a **plan**. |
When a thought keeps coming back, | ask, | "What's the next **step**?" |
Write it down, | with a **when** and a **where**. |
(warm) Then let your brain go off **duty**. ||
Rule **two**: | keep a worry **receipt**. |
Write tonight's worry **down**. |
Check it in a **week**. |
Did it **happen**? ↗ |
Most of the time, | the answer will be **no**. |
And your brain needs to see that in **writing**. |
(smile) Your worry has a **terrible** track record. | Make it show its **receipts**. ||
Rule **three**: | talk to yourself by **name**. |
(character) "Okay, **Sam**. | What would you tell a **friend** right now?" |
(smile) It sounds **strange**. | It works better than it **sounds**. ||
Rule **four**: | decide what's good **enough**. |
For small choices, | set a bar **before** you start. |
The first option that clears it **wins**. |
(smile) A good dinner tonight | beats a perfect dinner at ten at **night**. ||
And rule **five**: | give your mind a real **job**. |
Rumination loves an **idle** mind. |
Take a **walk**. | Call **someone**. | Cook **something**. |
Then come back to the problem, | if it still **needs** you. ||

## 8 · Twist and ending (8:20–9:10)
**Direction:** soft and generous. "Overthinkers aren't broken" is the heart of it. The "ok." callback is
gentle and a little funny. The challenge is bright, and the last two lines are a warm sign-off. Energy 2.

(slower) So here's the **twist**. |
Overthinkers aren't **broken**. |
(warm) Very often, | they're the most **careful** people you know. |
They remember **details**. |
They think about how others **feel**. |
They see problems coming before anyone **else** does. |
(smile) The same mind that replays an awkward text | remembers your best friend's **coffee** order. |
That's a real **strength**. ||
(land it) Thinking isn't the **problem**. |
Thinking in **circles** is. ||
(warm) So the next time your phone just says | "**ok**," | take a breath. |
They're probably just **busy**. | (smile) Or **asleep**. |
And you can be **too**. ||
(brisk) So here's your **challenge** for this week. |
Write down **one** worry tonight. |
Then check it in seven **days**. |
Come back and tell me if it came **true**. ||
(lean in) What's the one thing you've overthought the **most**? ↗ |
Tell me in the **comments**. ||
(warm) Which habit should we cover **next**? ↗ |
Drop it in the **comments**. ||
