Four moves. Use them next week.

Move one. The mood check. Before you hit "buy," ask one question: am I buying the thing... or the feeling? Just naming the feeling takes some of its power away. If the answer is "the feeling," close the app and get that sense of control somewhere free. Remember, the relief came from control, not from the stuff. Tidy one room. Finish one small task. Go for a walk. Call someone. Ten minutes of anything you choose.

Move two. The twenty-four-hour cart. Put it in the cart, close the tab, decide tomorrow. A good deal will usually still be there. And if the timer magically resets... well, now you know.

Move three. Fun money, decided in advance. Pick a Black Friday budget today, while you're calm. Then spend it, guilt-free. Planned fun is still fun. It just doesn't come with regret.

Move four. Shop the list, not the deal. Write down what you actually need before the sales start. If it's on the list and it's on sale, that's a win. If it's not on the list, it's not a deal. It's a trick with a discount.

Back to Bean. Same rough week. Black Friday arrives. The timer's ticking. Ninety-nine dollars, was two hundred.

But this time, Bean checks in. "Am I buying the jacket... or the feeling?" He puts it in the cart, and calls a friend instead. The next day, he buys the two things on his list, with the fun money he set aside. Nothing else.

The bad week still happened. But this time, it didn't come with a bill.

And here's the part nobody says about Black Friday. The best deal is often the one you didn't make. Every dollar Bean didn't spend went exactly where we put it in the last video... into the account called "Car Breaks."

So here's the truth. Shopping isn't the enemy. Shopping to fix a feeling is. Your mood changes what you'll pay, the joy peaks before the box arrives, and Black Friday is designed to catch you at exactly that moment.

Check the feeling. Wait a day. Spend the fun money you planned. Shop the list.
