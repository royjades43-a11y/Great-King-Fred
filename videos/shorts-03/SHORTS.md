# YouTube Shorts: "The Psychology of People Who Rewatch the Same Shows"

Seven vertical Shorts (1080×1920, 30 fps) built on the safe-zone layout (`videos/shorts/layout/SHORTS-LAYOUT.md`):
the hook title sits under YouTube's top bar, the picture and captions stay left of the like/comment
buttons and above the channel name and title. Covers are in `thumbnails/`.

On every Short, set **Related video** to the full video. It puts a tappable link under the Short and
is the main way Shorts send viewers to the long video.

Post one a day, strongest first: 04 → 01 → 03 → 07 → 02 → 06 → 05.

Keywords come from common search phrasing for this topic, not live search-volume data. Check them in
vidIQ or YouTube's search autocomplete before publishing and swap in stronger phrases you find.

**Title:** Nostalgia used to be a disease 😳

**Description:**
```
Nostalgia was once a medical diagnosis. In 1688, a Swiss medical student named Johannes Hofer described homesick soldiers who stopped eating and sleeping, and named it "nostalgia": homecoming + pain.

The cures? Leeches, opium, or a ticket home. American Civil War doctors were still diagnosing it.

**Description:**
```
Why does familiar feel good? In 1968, psychologist Robert Zajonc showed people made-up words and symbols. The more often they saw something, the more they liked it, even pure nonsense. It's called the mere exposure effect.

Then Blue's Clues aired the same episode five days in a row, and kids understood more each time.

## Why they're built this way

- **Hook from frame 0:** the two-line title is on screen from the first frame, so the Short has a
  strong preview even if YouTube picks a frame instead of your cover.
- **Captions show the real spelling:** numbers as digits ($425 million, 1951) and real names
  (Desi, Zajonc, Hofer), even though the voice read some of them spelled out.
- **Each Short opens on a clean sentence** and ends on a payoff line, so it works without the long video.
- **Descriptions** put the main search phrase in the first sentence, point to the related video, ask
  one question, and end with exactly three hashtags.
