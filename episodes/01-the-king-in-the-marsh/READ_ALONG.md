# The King in the Marsh - read-along script

About 672 words, roughly 4.5 minutes at documentary pace.

Record **one file per scene**, named exactly as shown (`.mp3`, `.wav` or `.m4a`), and put them in this episode's `voice/` folder. Or record the whole thing as a single `voice/full.mp3`, leaving about two seconds of silence between scenes.

Leave a natural pause between sentences; the visuals are timed to them. Don't read the scene headings.

## Pronunciation

- **Wessex**: WESS-ex
- **Athelney**: ATH-ul-nee
- **Guthrum**: GOOTH-rum
- **Athelstan**: ATH-ul-stan
- **Chippenham**: CHIP-en-um
- **Edington**: ED-ing-tun
- **Aller**: AL-er, rhymes with pallor
- **burhs**: burrs (Old English for fortified towns)

In the winter of eight seventy-eight, the King of Wessex vanished. His army was scattered. His kingdom was overrun. And the man history would one day call "the Great"... was hiding in a swamp.

*Delivery: low and unhurried; let the last word of each line land*

In a peasant's hut, in disguise, being scolded by a farmer's wife for burning her bread. This is the story of how England very nearly never happened.

## 3. `heathen_army.mp3`

*Delivery: a touch quicker and clipped; lean on the verbs*

Thirteen years earlier, a Viking force landed in East Anglia. The English called it the Great Heathen Army. It took York. It killed the king of East Anglia. It drove the king of Mercia into exile. One by one, the old kingdoms fell, until only one was left standing. Wessex.

## 4. `young_alfred.mp3`

*Delivery: low and unhurried; let the last word of each line land*

Its king was Alfred. The youngest of five brothers, a sickly, bookish boy who was never meant to rule. But his brothers died, one after another, and in eight seventy-one, barely into his twenties, the crown fell to him. That year he fought nine battles against the Danes. He won some. He lost more. And in the end, he made peace, and the Danes withdrew. For now.

## 5. `twelfth_night.mp3`

*Delivery: a touch quicker and clipped; lean on the verbs*

The peace did not last. On Twelfth Night, eight seventy-eight, Alfred was keeping Christmas at his royal estate at Chippenham. Out of the snow came a Viking army, led by a warlord named Guthrum. They struck in the dead of winter, when no one fights. The king escaped into the night with a handful of men.

## 6. `the_flight.mp3`

*Delivery: slow and quiet; leave space after each sentence*

Wessex collapsed almost overnight. Some of its nobles fled across the sea. Others bent the knee to the Danes. Alfred went south-west, into the flooded wetlands of the Somerset Levels, to a scrap of dry ground called Athelney. The Isle of Princes.

## 7. `the_marsh.mp3`

*Delivery: slow and quiet; leave space after each sentence*

Here, for months, the king lived like an outlaw. He and his few loyal men hid among the reeds, raiding Danish outposts, and vanishing back into the mist.

## 8. `the_cakes.mp3`

*Delivery: low and unhurried; let the last word of each line land*

And it is here that England's most famous legend takes place. Sheltering in a swineherd's cottage, the disguised king was asked to watch the loaves baking by the fire. Lost in thought about his ruined kingdom, he let them burn. The swineherd's wife, who had no idea who he was, gave the King of Wessex a furious telling-off. The story was first written down a century later. It may never have happened. But it remembers something true. Alfred had lost everything.

## 9. `egberts_stone.mp3`

*Delivery: warmer, lifting; a hint of surprise*

In May, seven weeks after Easter, Alfred rode out of the marsh, to a place called Egbert's Stone. He had sent word ahead. And they came. The men of Somerset, of Wiltshire, and of Hampshire. A chronicler wrote that they greeted him as if he had come back from the dead.

## 10. `edington.mp3`

*Delivery: driving and stronger; short sentences hit hard*

Two days later, at Edington, the armies met. Shield wall against shield wall. Alfred's men held. Then they pushed. And the Great Army broke. The Danes fled to their stronghold, and Alfred besieged them there. Fourteen days later, starving, they surrendered.

## 11. `the_font.mp3`

*Delivery: warmer, lifting; a hint of surprise*

And then Alfred did something no one expected. He did not kill Guthrum. He became his godfather. Three weeks later, at Aller, the Viking warlord was baptised a Christian, with Alfred lifting him from the font. Guthrum took a new name, Athelstan, and swore to stay out of Wessex. And this time, for the most part, he kept his word.

## 12. `two_kingdoms.mp3`

*Delivery: full voice but measured; savour the final line*

Wessex survived. Britain was split in two. The Danes in the north and east, the English in the south and west. And Alfred spent the rest of his reign making sure it would never fall so easily again.

## 13. `the_burhs.mp3`

*Delivery: full voice but measured; savour the final line*

He built a network of fortified towns, called burhs, so that almost no one in Wessex lived more than a day's march from safety. He reorganised the army. He built ships. And he had books translated into English, so that his people could read them in their own language.

## 14. `the_great.mp3`

*Delivery: full voice but measured; savour the final line*

His grandson, Athelstan, would become the first king of all England. But it began here, with a hunted king in a swamp, who refused to give up. That is why, of all the kings and queens of England, Alfred is the only one we still call the Great.
