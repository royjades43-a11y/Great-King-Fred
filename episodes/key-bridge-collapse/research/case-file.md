## One-line logline

A single control wire was never seated all the way into its terminal block, because its printed label sleeve had been fitted over the ferrule's collar when the ship was built in 2015. On March 26, 2024 it came loose and blacked out the 984-foot containership Dali 3,200 feet from the Key Bridge, whose piers could not survive a hit from a ship that size. Four minutes and nine seconds later the ship struck Pier 17. Thirteen seconds after that the pier gave way, and six highway workers died.

**Story spine:** loose wire (probable cause) -> blackout -> engine shutdown and no steering -> no time to recover (contributing) -> unprotected bridge never assessed for ship-strike risk (contributing to the collapse and deaths) -> workers never warned (contributing to the deaths).

## Key facts

### The event
- Date and time: March 26, 2024, impact at **0129:09 eastern daylight time** [MIR p.47; p.23 fn.4: "all times are eastern daylight time"]. Preliminary report gave 0129:10 [Prelim p.12] (see Open questions).
- Location: Fort McHenry Channel, Patapsco River, Baltimore, Maryland. The Dali's starboard bow struck the **northwest column of Pier 17**, the southern pier of the central span [MIR p.47; p.xii].
- Impact speed: **6.4 knots (7.4 mph)** [MIR p.47].
- Impact force: estimated at **over four times** Pier 17's lateral capacity of **5,509 kips** (FHWA calculation) [MIR p.48-49, p.57-58].
- First contact about **20 feet above the waterline** [MIR p.49].
- Pier 17 collapsed at **0129:22**, 13 seconds after impact. Spans 17-22 and Piers 19, 20, 21 went down [MIR p.49, p.58].
- Time from first blackout to impact: **4 minutes 9 seconds** (0125:00 to 0129:09) [MIR p.35, p.221-226].

### Casualties
- On the bridge: a seven-person Brawner Builders road maintenance crew (repairing potholes) plus one inspector from Eborn Enterprises, Inc., a subconsultant to MDTA [MIR p.26].
- **Six highway workers died.** One survived with serious injuries. The inspector escaped unharmed [MIR p.xii, p.56 Table 1].
- On the Dali: 21 crew and 2 pilots (23 people). One crewmember (an able seafarer on the bow) had minor injuries (a laceration and inhalation injuries) [MIR p.23, p.49, p.56].
- All six victims were found inside vehicles. Two were recovered March 27; the other four were located between April 5 and May 7 [MIR p.51].

### The ship
- *Dali*: Singapore-flagged containership, **983.9 ft (299.9 m)** long, 158.1 ft (48.2 m) beam, built **2015** by **HD Hyundai Heavy Industries (HHI)** [MIR p.77-78 Table 2].
- Owner: Grace Ocean Pte Ltd (bought in 2016). Manager and operator: **Synergy Marine Pte Ltd**. Class: **ClassNK** (since February 2017; ABS before that) [MIR p.77-78].
- 95,128 GT. Single Hyundai-MAN B&W 9S90ME-C9.2 engine, **55,626 hp (41,480 kW)**, direct drive [MIR p.78].
- Loaded with **4,679 containers** (8,544 TEU), 55,671 metric tons of cargo, bound for **Colombo, Sri Lanka** on a 27-day voyage [MIR p.26]. 56 containers held dangerous goods; 14 of those were damaged [MIR p.52].
- Draft 12.15 m (39.86 ft). Displacement as loaded: 112,383 metric tons [MIR p.28].
- Power: four diesel generators feeding a **6,600-volt HV bus**, plus a **440-volt LV bus** fed through two step-down transformers (TR1/TR2) with breakers HR1/HR2 (HV side) and LR1/LR2 (LV side) [MIR p.80].

### The bridge
- Francis Scott Key Bridge: steel continuous through-truss carrying Maryland 695 over the Patapsco from Baltimore to Dundalk. Owned and operated by the **Maryland Transportation Authority (MDTA)** [MIR p.26, p.60].
- **Opened March 23, 1977.** About **9,086 feet** long between abutments. Vertical clearance over the 700-ft Fort McHenry Federal Channel: **185 feet** [MIR p.60].
- 37 piers; ships passed between Piers 17 and 18 [MIR p.47 fn.40]. Clear space between them: 1,100 ft. Channel width: 700 ft. **Only 200 feet** from the channel edge to Pier 17 or 18 [MIR p.67].
- Designed to the **1969 AASHTO** specifications, which "did not mention the risk of vessel strikes" [MIR p.63-64].
- Protection: four dolphins, each about **489 feet** from the pier it guarded, plus crushable concrete and timber fenders at Piers 17 and 18. **The Dali hit none of the dolphins** [MIR p.64-67].
- Designed with nonredundant steel tension members (formerly called "fracture critical"). NTSB found this was **not** a factor [MIR p.60; Finding 1, p.199].
- 2023 average daily traffic: **34,121 vehicles**, 10% trucks. It was the main route for hazardous-materials trucks, which are barred from the harbor tunnels [MIR p.63, p.xii].
- Inspections in March 2021 and May 2023 rated deck, superstructure and substructure satisfactory and the pier protection "in place and functioning properly" [MIR p.69].

### Costs and recovery
- Damage to the Dali: more than **$18 million**. Cargo damage undetermined [MIR p.57].
- Bridge replacement estimate: **$4.3 billion to $5.2 billion**; expected to open in **late 2030** [MIR p.xii, p.59].
- About **50,000 tons** of material removed from the riverbed [MIR p.55].

## Causal chain

### Probable cause (verbatim) [MIR p.202]
> "The National Transportation Safety Board determines that the probable cause of the contact of the containership Dali with the Francis Scott Key Bridge was a loss of electrical power (blackout), due to a loose signal wire connection to a terminal block stemming from the improper installation of wire-label banding, resulting in the vessel's loss of propulsion and steering close to the bridge."

### Contributing factors (verbatim, same paragraph) [MIR p.202]
1. "Contributing to the crew's inability to recover propulsion from the loss of electrical power was the limited time available due to the Dali's proximity to the bridge."
2. "Contributing to the collapse of the Francis Scott Key Bridge and the loss of life was the lack of countermeasures to reduce the bridge's vulnerability to collapse due to impact by ocean-going vessels, which could have been implemented if a vulnerability assessment had been conducted by the Maryland Transportation Authority as recommended by the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials."
3. "Also contributing to the loss of life was the lack of effective and immediate communications to notify the highway workers to evacuate the bridge."

### The chain, link by link
1. **Built in.** At HHI (2015), each control wire in the HV switchboard got a heat-shrunk label band printed with its terminal block number. On Wire 1 the band **covered the whole blue insulated collar of the ferrule**. That made it too fat to seat fully in its WAGO 280-681 spring-clamp terminal block (Terminal Block 381) [MIR p.146-147; Finding 3, p.199]. HHI had "no specific materials, instructions, or training" for installing HV switchboard wiring, and its quality check was "shaking off the lines" [MIR p.98-99].
2. **Hidden for years.** Wire 1 sat in the control circuit that keeps breaker HR1 closed (it feeds 110 V DC to HR1's undervoltage release). The ferrule tip rested on the clamp gate and made an intermittent contact. The NTSB lab found **arcing damage** on both the gate and the ferrule [MIR p.127, p.144]. TR1/HR1 had not been in use for at least 7 months, so nobody would have seen the problem [MIR p.145].
3. **Missed by inspection.** The 6-month switchboard inspection was signed off on **March 23, 2024, three days before the accident**, with no abnormalities [MIR p.99]. NTSB found that infrared thermal imaging "may have" caught the loose wire [Finding 4, p.199].
4. **Switched into service.** After the March 25 in-port blackouts, the electrician moved the load onto TR1, putting HR1 and the loose wire back in the circuit [MIR p.95].
5. **The wire lets go.** At 0125:00, Wire 1 disconnects, the UVR de-energizes and HR1 opens. To the system this "was as if an operator had rotated the breaker control switch," so **no alarm** sounded [MIR p.144]. The LV bus goes dark [Finding 2, p.199].
6. **Engine shuts itself off.** Loss of the cooling-water pump triggered an automatic main engine shutdown on low cooling-water pressure. This was an as-built configuration that met class rules but "endangered the vessel" [Finding 6, p.199]. The engine never restarted [MIR p.39].
7. **Steering lost and slow to recover.** All three steering pumps lost power. The rudder stayed midships for 58 s, until the manual breaker reset. Breakers were in Manual; in Automatic mode the gap would have been about **10 s** [Finding 12, p.200]. The emergency generator took **70 s** instead of 45 s because a radiator damper limit switch did not read "open" in time [Finding 13, p.200].
8. **The ship turns toward the pier.** With the rudder midships and no propulsion, the ship swung to starboard. The NTSB discusses asymmetric bank effect where the Curtis Bay Channel joins (shallower water to port, dredged channel to starboard) [MIR p.164-165].
9. **Second blackout.** The fuel "flushing pump," which the crew had been using as the **only fuel supply** for DG3/DG4 (a long-standing jury-rig; the designed supply and booster pumps were isolated), did not auto-restart. The generators starved and at 0127:04 the ship lost all HV and LV power [Findings 9-10, p.199-200; MIR p.94]. Synergy's oversight was "inadequate" [Finding 11, p.200]. **This was not causal to the first blackout** [MIR p.xiv].
10. **No time.** The pilots' and crew's actions were "executed in a timely manner" but were "rendered ineffective" by the lack of propulsion [Finding 14, p.200]. The anchor brake would not open at first. The bow thruster was dead. The tug was 16 minutes away [MIR p.167].
11. **A bridge that could not take the hit.** The Dali's force was more than four times Pier 17's capacity [MIR p.57-58]. The dolphins were too far out to help. The NTSB's AASHTO Method II calculation gave the Key Bridge an annual frequency of collapse of **0.002921**, almost **30 times** the 0.0001 threshold for a critical/essential bridge [MIR p.135, Table 5]. MDTA had never run a vulnerability assessment and was not required to [MIR p.132].
12. **Nobody told the workers.** Police stopped traffic 48 s before impact, but procedures for real-time communication to the crew did not exist. The police "did not call the inspector" despite having his number [MIR p.177]. If the workers had been warned when police were, they would have had **1 min 16 s before impact (1 min 29 s before collapse)** to drive south to safety at Span 16 [MIR p.177-178; Finding 18, p.201].

### Ruled out [Finding 1, MIR p.199]
Environmental or waterway conditions. Vessel manning and credentials. Alcohol or drugs. Fuel quality or fuel switchover. The ship's ability to get underway after the in-port blackouts. The nonredundant steel tension members in the truss.

## Warnings ignored and near misses

- **1980 Blue Nagoya strike.** A 390-ft Japanese containership lost steering about 600 yards out and hit Pier 17's fender. Its displacement was about one-tenth of the Dali's. The fender stopped it, was destroyed, and was **rebuilt to the original design** [MIR p.72-73]. NTSB press release: "The Dali, however, is 10 times the size of the Blue Nagoya" [NTSB PR 2025-11-18].
- **1980 Sunshine Skyway** (35 dead) led the NTSB to recommend pier protection standards (M-81-20). That produced AASHTO's 1991 Guide Specification, which recommended **assessing existing pre-1991 bridges** [MIR p.131-132].
- **March 2006 Harbor Safety and Coordination Committee meeting:** an **MDTA representative stated that the Key Bridge "was not designed to withstand contact by a large vessel."** Pier protection then appeared as an action item in the minutes from 2006 on [MIR p.134].
- **The day before.** The Dali had **two blackouts in port on March 25**. The second had the same root as the second underway blackout (the flushing pump) [MIR p.xiv, p.94-95].
- **Known jury-rig.** A January 2024 email from the previous chief engineer, with Synergy's technical manager copied, said the DG3/DG4 supply system was "not in use all pumps heaters etc isolated after piping modification and permanently kept on LSMGO [flushing pump] line" [MIR p.94].
- **Ship growth.** Since 1977, and with the 2016 Panama Canal expansion, much larger vessels have been using Baltimore [MIR p.135].

## Aftermath

### NTSB recommendations
- **March 18, 2025 (MIR-25-10), urgent:**
  - H-25-1 and H-25-2: FHWA, with the Coast Guard and USACE, to form an interdisciplinary team that helps bridge owners.
  - H-25-3 and H-25-4: **30 owners of 68 bridges** in 19 states to calculate the AASHTO Method II collapse risk and, if it is over the threshold, build risk-reduction plans [MIR p.xxi, p.207-208; NTSB PR 2025-03-20].
- **November 2025 (MIR-25-40), new:** M-25-16 to M-25-29 and H-25-28 to H-25-31 [MIR p.203-207]. They include:
  - Coast Guard to study redundant propulsion and steering for single-engine ships (M-25-16).
  - Coast Guard to push IMO to fix the ISM Code's safety management system requirements (M-25-17).
  - VDR fixes through the IMO and IEC: record during blackouts, record engine room phone audio, separate microphone channels (M-25-18, M-25-20).
  - Motorist warning systems: FHWA (H-25-28), AASHTO (H-25-29), and 20 bridge owners (H-25-30).
  - Emergency communications for workers on bridges (H-25-31).
  - WAGO to add a warning to the 280-681 data sheet (M-25-22).
  - HHI to fix its wire-banding procedures (M-25-23).
  - Synergy to adopt thermal imaging, keep breakers in Automatic, and stop using the flushing pump as a service pump (M-25-25 to M-25-29).
- Board Member Michael Graham filed a concurring statement: the ISM Code's risk management is "entirely reactive" [MIR p.209].

### Operational changes noted in the report
- The pilots' association now has dispatchers call MDTA first, then the Coast Guard [MIR p.42 fn.36].
- Synergy instituted twice-yearly transformer switching [MIR p.145 fn.133].
- McAllister added an ABS-classed escort tug, the Isabel McAllister [MIR p.34 fn.26].
- Synergy told crews not to use the flushing pump as a standalone fuel pump. As of the report, this was **not yet formal company policy** [MIR p.99].

### Other investigations
- The **Coast Guard Marine Board of Investigation** was convened March 26, 2024. Its report was not available when MIR-25-40 was published [MIR p.211].
- The Coast Guard's Ports and Waterways Safety Board of Inquiry (chartered May 15, 2024) was likewise pending [MIR p.212].

### Rebuild
- Cable-stayed replacement: 2 miles long, at least 230 ft of clearance, 1,600 ft between pylons, pier protection per the latest AASHTO specs. Estimated $4.3-5.2 billion, opening late 2030 [MIR p.74, p.59].

### Legal (secondary sources; not in the NTSB report)
- **Sep 18, 2024:** the US Department of Justice sued Grace Ocean and Synergy for negligence and alleged "jury-rigging." On **Oct 24, 2024**, it settled for **$101.9 million** [WP]. Some outlets report $102 million.
- Brawner Builders, Chubb/Ace American (seeking $350 million), the families of the six victims, the survivor, the inspector, longshoremen and the State of Maryland all sued [WP].
- **2026 (UNVERIFIED against primary DOJ documents; based on secondary reporting found by web search):**
  - May 2026: reported federal criminal charges against Synergy-related companies and a technical superintendent, and a reported **$2.25 billion** Maryland settlement.
  - June 2026: a deferred prosecution agreement for the chief engineer under the Ports and Waterways Safety Act over the fuel-supply arrangement [WP; Insurance Journal 2026-06-19; Equipment World].
  - Verify on justice.gov before using on screen.

## Open questions and disputed points

- **Ship length:** the preliminary report says **947 ft** [Prelim p.1, p.13]; the final report says **984 ft / 983.9 ft overall** [MIR p.78]. Use the final figure. 947 ft is likely a different length measure, but the report does not explain the difference.
- **IMO number:** the preliminary report gives **9697428** [Prelim p.1, p.24]; MIR-25-40 Table 2 gives **9431848** [MIR p.78]. This is unresolved. Do not show either on screen without checking a registry.
- **Impact time and speed:** preliminary 0129:10 at 6.5 kn [Prelim p.12]; final **0129:09 at 6.4 kn** [MIR p.47]. Use the final.
- **Distance at first blackout:** preliminary "0.6 miles—or three ship lengths" [Prelim p.9]; final "about 3,200 feet (3.3 ship lengths)" [MIR p.35].
- **Blue Nagoya year:** the MIR-25-40 section heading says "1977 Contact" (p.72 and table of contents), but the text says **1980**. The NTSB press release also says 1980. Use 1980.
- **Bridge length:** the NTSB press release calls it "2.37-mile-long" [NTSB PR 2025-11-18]. The report says about **9,086 ft (about 1.7 mi)** [MIR p.60]. Use the report.
- **When Wire 1 backed out:** the NTSB "could not determine whether Wire 1 backed out ... before or at the time of the initial underway blackout" [MIR p.145].
- **Emergency generator delay:** the damper limit switch failed "due to unknown circumstances" [Finding 13, MIR p.200].
- **Oil sheen source:** the Coast Guard suspected the bow thruster, but ClassNK found no mechanical damage there. "The source of the sheen was never confirmed" [MIR p.53].
- **Engine room voices:** the VDR did not record the engine room phone or isolate microphones, so the engine room's side of the crisis is partly unknown [Finding 22-23, MIR p.201].
- **In-port vs underway blackouts:** in May 2024, Homendy called the in-port and voyage outages "mechanically distinct" [WP]. The final report links only the **second** blackouts (the flushing pump) to March 25 [MIR p.xiv].
- **Settlement figure:** $101.9M per Wikipedia, $102M or $103M in other outlets.
- **2026 criminal case and $2.25B settlement:** secondary sources only. UNVERIFIED.
- **Coast Guard Marine Board report:** not public when the NTSB published. Check for later release.
