# Episode Plan: The Key Bridge Collapse

**Working title:** *One Loose Wire: Why the Key Bridge Fell*
**Alt titles:** *Four Minutes and Nine Seconds* / *The Wire and the Bridge*
**Runtime target:** about 10:00 (600 s), about 1,450 spoken words at 145 wpm
**Source of truth:** `research/case-file.md` (NTSB MIR-25-40, final, Nov 18, 2025). Citations below use the case-file key: `[MIR p.N]` = MIR-25-40 *printed* page (PDF page = printed + 2).

**Hook line (cold open):** "At 1:25 in the morning on March 26th, 2024, every light on a 984-foot ship went dark, 3,200 feet from a bridge that no one had ever asked how hard it could be hit."

---

## The spine (what this episode argues)

1. **The ship (probable cause):** a label band fitted too far down one signal wire at Terminal Block 381 when the Dali was built in 2015 meant the wire was never fully seated. On March 26, 2024 it let go, a breaker opened with no alarm, the ship blacked out, the engine shut itself off and the steering died. 4 min 9 s later the ship hit Pier 17 [MIR p.202, p.xiii, p.35].
2. **The bridge (the second-act twist):** ships lose power. Bridges are not supposed to fall when they do. The Key Bridge was designed to 1969 rules that never mentioned ship strikes; MDTA never ran the AASHTO vulnerability assessment; had it, the risk would have come out at almost **30 times** the threshold [MIR p.63-64, p.132, p.135].
3. **The warning (the human cost):** police stopped traffic 48 s before impact, but nobody had an effective way to tell the road crew [MIR p.47, p.177; Finding 18, p.201].

The episode must **not** land on "a wire did it". The cause act ends on the wire, then turns: *the wire explains why the ship hit the bridge; it does not explain why six men died.*

## Ground rules for the writer and fact-checker

- **Figures (final report wins):** ship length **984 ft** (not 947); impact **0129:09 at 6.4 knots** (not 0129:10 / 6.5); first blackout at **about 3,200 ft / 3.3 ship lengths**; bridge length **about 9,086 ft (1.7 mi)** (not "2.37 miles"); Blue Nagoya strike **1980** (ignore the "1977 Contact" heading).
- **Do not show an IMO number** on screen (unresolved discrepancy).
- **Do not name the shipyard location**, crew or pilots. Roles only (senior pilot, electrician, etc.).
- **The six workers:** respectful, never graphic. No imagery of vehicles being recovered or of victims. They were on a break in their vehicles [MIR p.49]. Name only the four names the case file lists from news sources; **do not name the other two** (UNVERIFIED; one family asked for privacy per [WP]).
- **Legal:** no 2026 criminal charges, deferred prosecution or $2.25B settlement on screen or in narration (UNVERIFIED). The Oct 2024 DOJ $101.9M settlement is secondary-source; it is left out of this cut. If a producer wants it back, it must be labelled "according to court filings" and verified on justice.gov first.
- **Second blackout:** it was caused by the flushing-pump fuel jury-rig and is **not causal to the first** [MIR p.xiv]. Keep that distinction clear.
- **When Wire 1 let go** is uncertain ("before or at the time of" the blackout) [MIR p.145]: say "at or just before".
- Docket video contents are **unviewed**; the footage scout must confirm what each clip shows before the edit relies on it.

## Act 2: The world before (0:40-1:55)

**b07** · footage · 9 s
- Narration: the Key Bridge opened on March 23, 1977, carrying Maryland 695 over the Patapsco River, owned and run by the Maryland Transportation Authority [MIR p.60, p.26].
- Shots: S10 (1977 opening / construction-era photo); fallback S38 (MIR Fig 2, bridge in 2004).

**b08** · footage · 14 s
- Narration: a steel through-truss about 9,086 feet long, 185 feet over the shipping channel. In 2023 it carried about 34,000 vehicles a day, and it was the main route for hazardous-materials trucks barred from the harbor tunnels [MIR p.60, p.63, p.xii].
- Shots: S11 (intact bridge, 2015); S12 (NTSB B-roll aerial, wide); S47 (traffic on the bridge).

**b09** · footage · 9 s
- Narration (plant for Act 5): ships passed between Piers 17 and 18. From the edge of the channel to either pier: only 200 feet [MIR p.47 fn.40, p.67].
- Shots: S21 (Commons, Pier 17 southern truss support, 2016); S35 (locator map) with the channel drawn in.

**b10** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: the Dali: Singapore-flagged, 984 feet, built in 2015 by HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, run by Synergy Marine [MIR p.77-78 Table 2].
- Shots: S34 (Dali portrait, rights-checked); S45 (container-ship size comparison) as alt.

**b11** · footage · 10 s
- Narration (plant for the cause): one engine, one propeller, one rudder. The engine's pumps, the steering and the lights all ran on electricity from four diesel generators, through switchboards full of control wiring [MIR p.78, p.80].
- Shots: S23 (MIR Fig 42, HV and LV switchboards); S33 (MIR Fig 4, the Dali's helm at night).

**b12** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: loaded at Seagirt Marine Terminal: 4,679 containers, bound for Colombo, Sri Lanka, 27 days away [MIR p.25-26].
- Shots: S41 (Seagirt / Baltimore harbor cranes); S32 (docket "1 Bridge March 23 2024" in-port clip, if it shows the ship).

**b13** · footage · 13 s
- Narration: on the bridge that night, a Brawner Builders crew was filling potholes. The right southbound lane was closed. Police units sat at each end for traffic control. An inspector was walking the span checking the cement [MIR p.26, p.49].
- Shots: S16 (MIR Fig 17, crew vehicle positions, as a quiet diagram build); S12 (night or dusk aerial of the deck). No faces, no vehicles identifiable.

## Act 4: The investigation (4:45-6:05)

**b33** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: the NTSB was on scene the same day, led by Chair Jennifer Homendy. The ship sat pinned under hundreds of tons of steel [MIR p.211].
- Shots: S13 (NTSB Flickr photos); S12 (NTSB B-roll aerial).

**b34** · evidence · 12 s
- Narration: the data recorder had gone blind at 0125:03. Investigators rebuilt the last four minutes from VDR audio, CCTV and alarm logs, second by second [MIR p.37; Appendix C, p.221-226].
- Shots: evidence board: S64 (Table C-1 excerpt), S01 still, S22 diagram stills, red string between the times.

**b35** · evidence · 12 s
- Narration: the second blackout had an answer: for months the crew had fed two generators from a "flushing" pump, a jury-rig an email in January had described. But that only explained the second blackout, not the first [MIR p.94, p.xiv; Findings 9-11, p.199-200].
- Shots: evidence board: S22 (fuel-system diagram), card with the January 2024 email line (verbatim from [MIR p.94]), a red "NOT THE FIRST" note.

**b36** · document · 11 s
- Narration: the first blackout started when breaker HR1 opened with no alarm. "It was as if an operator had rotated the breaker control switch to 'open'" [MIR p.144].
- Shots: S63 (document card, quotable line 3); S26 (MIR Fig 50, HR1 undervoltage circuit) behind.

**b37** · footage · 23 s
- Narration: on April 29, during testing, HR1 tripped again, on its own. Shipyard engineers traced it to one wire, Wire 1, in Terminal Block 381. Homendy: "Finding this single wire was like hunting for a loose rivet on the Eiffel Tower" [MIR p.113-115; NTSB PR 2025-11-18].
- Shots: S05 (MIR Fig 51, full reveal); S23 (switchboard wide, to show scale); S42 (Homendy at the Nov 18, 2025 board meeting).

**b38** · evidence · 12 s
- Narration: in mid-May the NTSB cut out the block and took it to the lab. X-rays, then microscopes: gouges, and arcing damage on both the wire's ferrule and the clamp [MIR p.117-127].
- Shots: S24 (MIR Fig 56, X-ray); S14 (MIR Figs 58-62, arcing close-ups) pinned with notes.

## Act 5: The cause (6:05-8:40)

**b39** · title · 5 s
- Narration: none. "2015 · the shipyard".
- Shots: S60.

**b40** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: when the Dali was built, every control wire got a heat-shrunk label printed with its terminal number. The yard had "no specific materials, instructions, or training" for this wiring; its quality check was "shaking off the lines" [MIR p.98-99].
- Shots: S23 (switchboard); S53 (MIR Figs 53-54, Wire 1 and Wire 3).

**b41** · document · 9 s
- Narration: quotable line 2: the label band "covered all of the ferrule's blue insulated collar ... preventing the wire from being fully inserted into its terminal block" [MIR p.xiii].
- Shots: S63 (document card; highlight "preventing the wire from being fully inserted").

**b42** · evidence · 12 s
- Narration: a few millimetres too far down. The fatter ferrule could not seat in the spring clamp; its tip rested on the clamp's gate [MIR p.146-147; Finding 3, p.199].
- Shots: S06 (MIR Fig 65, correct vs Wire 1 placement), animated slide of the band; S53.

**b43** · evidence · 19 s
- Narration: the wire made only an intermittent contact (the lab found arcing), and for years nobody saw it, partly because TR1 and HR1 sat unused for months at a time. The six-monthly switchboard check was signed off on March 23, 2024, three days before. Thermal imaging "may have" caught it. Then, on March 25, the electrician put HR1 back in service [MIR p.127, p.144-145, p.99, p.95; Finding 4, p.199].
- Shots: S25 (MIR Fig 64, arcing diagram); a pinned "MARCH 23 · NO ABNORMALITIES" note; S22 (TR1 switch).

**b44** · evidence · 12 s
- Narration: the chain: wire lets go, HR1 opens, low-voltage bus dark, cooling pump stops, engine shuts down, steering gone. With the breakers in Automatic, power would have been back in about 10 seconds, not 58 [MIR p.144; Findings 2, 6, 12, p.199-200].
- Shots: evidence board: red string linking S05, S26, S22 stills, S15.

**b45** · document · 10 s
- Narration: the probable cause, verbatim: "a loose signal wire connection to a terminal block stemming from the improper installation of wire-label banding" [MIR p.202].
- Shots: S63 (document card; highlight "improper installation of wire-label banding").

**b46** · title · 5 s
- Narration (the turn): "That is why the ship hit the bridge. It is not why the bridge fell." Card: "The second question".
- Shots: S60.

**b47** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: in 1980 a freighter hit Florida's Sunshine Skyway and 35 people died. That led, by 1991, to national guidance telling owners to assess older bridges for ship strikes [MIR p.131-132].
- Shots: S27 (MIR Fig 38 / Sunshine Skyway photos); S49 (Queen Isabella, I-40, Figs 39-40) as quick cuts.

**b48** · evidence · 11 s
- Narration: the same year, 1980, the 390-foot Blue Nagoya lost steering and hit Pier 17's fender. The fender stopped it, was destroyed, and was rebuilt to the original design. The Dali was ten times its size [MIR p.72-73; NTSB PR 2025-11-18].
- Shots: S07 (MIR Fig 36, Blue Nagoya vs Dali); S39 (MIR Figs 31-32, Pier 17 fender).

**b49** · document · 9 s
- Narration: March 2006, a harbor safety meeting. An MDTA representative said the Key Bridge "was not designed to withstand contact by a large vessel" [MIR p.134].
- Shots: S63 (document card, quotable line 7).

**b50** · evidence · 11 s
- Narration: MDTA never ran the assessment, and no rule forced it to. The NTSB ran it afterwards: an annual collapse risk of 0.0029, almost 30 times the limit for a critical bridge [MIR p.132, p.135 Table 5].
- Shots: S08 (MIR Table 5), the two numbers pulled out on the board in red.

**b51** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: the protective dolphins stood about 489 feet from the piers. The Dali touched none of them; the pier took the full blow [MIR p.64-67].
- Shots: S17 (MIR Fig 30, overhead with dolphins and channel marked); S43 (Aug 2024 aerial of the gap).

**b52** · footage · 12 s
- Narration: and the workers. The police had the inspector's number; they did not call it. Warned when the police were, the crew would have had 1 minute 16 seconds, enough to drive south to a span that stayed up [MIR p.177-178; Finding 18, p.201].
- Shots: S16 (MIR Fig 17, vehicle positions, with Span 16 marked); S12.

**b53** · evidence · 10 s
- Narration: three failures, three red strings: a wire never pushed home; a bridge never assessed; a crew never warned [MIR p.202].
- Shots: full evidence board pull-back: S05, S08, S16 pinned and linked.

## Act 6: Aftermath and lesson (8:40-10:00)

**b54** · footage · 22 s
- Narration: salvage: explosives cut the truss off the bow on May 13; the Dali was refloated May 20; the channel fully reopened June 10. About 50,000 tons came out of the river. Repaired in China, the Dali returned to service in January 2025 [MIR p.55, p.57].
- Shots: S18 (DVIDS controlled demolition); S19 (DVIDS refloat); S28 (Navy sonar wreck images); S43.

**b55** · footage · 12 s
- Narration: in March 2025 the NTSB issued urgent recommendations: 30 owners of 68 bridges in 19 states should run the same risk calculation [MIR p.xxi, p.207-208].
- Shots: S12 (aerial); S42 (NTSB briefing); a simple map card is acceptable.

**b56** · footage · 12 s
- Narration: the final report adds more: fix the yard's banding procedure, a warning on the terminal block's data sheet, thermal imaging on ships, a way to warn people working on bridges, and gates to stop drivers [MIR p.203-207].
- Shots: S31 (MIR Figs 71-72, motorist warning lights and gates); S06 reprise.

**b57** · footage · 12 s
- Narration: the NTSB found that the quick calls by the pilots, the dispatcher and the police kept the toll from being far worse [Finding 15, p.200]. Six men still died: Alejandro Hernández Fuentes, Dorlian Ronial Castillo Cabrera, Maynor Yassir Suazo Sandoval, Miguel Ángel Luna González, and two of their co-workers [WP; CBS Baltimore; case file "People"].
- Shots: S30 (Commons memorial). Writer: do not add the two unverified names; fact-checker confirms the four spellings.

**b58** · footage · 10 s
- Narration: the replacement, a cable-stayed bridge with at least 230 feet of clearance and pier protection built to current rules, is expected in late 2030 at $4.3 to $5.2 billion [MIR p.74, p.59].
- Shots: S20 (MIR Fig 37 / MDTA rendering).

**b59** · title · 12 s
- Narration (echo the cold open): at 1:25 a.m. one wire let go. It had been waiting nine years. The bridge had been waiting longer. Homendy: "like all of the accidents we investigate, this was preventable" [NTSB PR 2025-11-18].
- Shots: S60 ("01:25:00" card returns), fade to black.
