# Translating "The $5 Coffee Myth Is Wrong"

## The plan: one video, many languages
Upload **one** video and add other languages to it, rather than uploading separate copies. All views, likes and watch time then
stay on the same video, so it ranks better. YouTube lets you add three things per language:

| What | Effect | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Translated title and description** | The video appears in search and recommendations in that language | Ready below (Spanish, French, Portuguese, German) |
| **Subtitles** | Viewers can follow along; YouTube can also index the text | `en.srt`, `es.srt`, `fr.srt` ready |
| **Dubbed audio track** | Viewers hear the video in their language. This is what really reaches new audiences | You make it in ElevenLabs (steps below) |

## Which languages
Most of this video's examples are U.S.-specific (401(k), U.S. card rates, the Bureau of Labor Statistics), so start with languages
spoken widely in and around the U.S. market, then grow:

1. **Spanish:** the largest audience after English, including about 40+ million U.S. Spanish speakers who see the same U.S. examples. Do this first.
2. **Portuguese (Brazil):** very large and engaged YouTube audience; lower ad rates.
3. **French:** France, Canada and West/Central Africa; good ad rates in France and Canada.
4. **German:** smaller, but some of the highest ad rates in the world.
5. Later: **Hindi** (huge reach, low ad rates) and **Japanese** (high ad rates).

## Step 1: Dub the audio in ElevenLabs
Use **ElevenLabs Dubbing** (Dubbing Studio): it's built for this and keeps the same voice and roughly the same timing.
1. Upload `renders/coffee-myth-web.mp4` (or the voiceover parts) to Dubbing and choose the target language, for example Spanish.
2. Keep the **same voice**, so the channel sounds the same in every language.
3. Open the transcript editor and check the numbers read naturally, e.g. "ciento setenta y dos mil dólares".
   Correct anything with the subtitle files here as a reference: they are reviewed translations of the exact script.
4. Export **audio only** (MP3 or WAV) at the full video length (6:53).

Spanish and French usually run 15–25% longer than English. ElevenLabs Dubbing speeds up or condenses to fit, so check that
no sentence sounds rushed. If one does, shorten the wording of that line in the editor.

## Step 2: Upload to YouTube
In **YouTube Studio → Content → the video → Languages**:
1. Set the video's language to **English**.
2. **Add language** → Spanish. Then:
   - **Title and description:** paste the Spanish ones below.
   - **Subtitles:** Upload file → `es.srt` (with timing).
   - **Audio:** upload the dubbed Spanish track. This option appears if your channel has multi-language audio enabled; YouTube has been rolling it out gradually. If you don't see it, upload subtitles and titles now, and add audio later when it appears.
3. Repeat for French, then Portuguese and German.
4. Add `en.srt` as English subtitles too. Accurate captions help viewers watching with the sound off and help YouTube understand the video.

YouTube may also offer **automatic dubbing** in Studio. It's free and fast, but you can't control the voice or the wording;
the ElevenLabs version will sound much more like the channel.

## What stays in English
The on-screen labels (price tags, receipts, chapter stamps) stay in English. With subtitles and a dubbed voice that works fine for
most viewers. If one language takes off (say Spanish), I can render a fully localized version with translated on-screen text later.
