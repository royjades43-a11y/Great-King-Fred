## How big can it get?

Animated psychology / "why do people…" explainers are one of the largest evergreen niches on
YouTube: the leading channels sit in the millions of subscribers, and individual videos on
everyday-behaviour questions routinely pass 1M views years after upload, because people search
these questions. Realistic targets for a consistent solo channel with this format:

| Stage | Output | Realistic range | What decides it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 months | 12–15 long videos + Shorts | 0 → 1k subs | Finding the 1–2 topics that get clicked |
| 3–12 months | 1 long / week + 3–5 Shorts / week | 1k → 10–50k | One video breaking out, then repeating its shape |
| Year 2 | same cadence, better packaging | 50k → 250k+ | Series, CTR above ~6%, retention above ~45% |
| Year 3+ | team (writer / animator) | 250k → 1M+ possible | Volume without losing quality |

These are ranges, not promises: most of the outcome is title + thumbnail + first 30 seconds.

## Step by step

1. **Positioning (week 1).** One sentence: *"The real reason behind the things people do — in 8
   minutes, with a stickman."* Banner, avatar and every thumbnail in the same paper/ink/red look
   as this video, so a viewer recognises you in a feed.
2. **Topic bank (week 1).** 50 titles, all a "Why do/don't people…" or "The psychology of…"
   question a normal person has asked. Start with the ones that hurt or embarrass:
   apologising, overthinking, ghosting, people-pleasing, envy, procrastination, staying in bad
   relationships, why you replay cringe memories, why kind people get used, why smart people
   believe dumb things. Check each in YouTube search autocomplete; keep the ones it completes.
3. **Format (every video).** 6–10 minutes. Hook in the first 5 s (a "you" situation, like "You
   were wrong. You know you were wrong."), name the payoff by 30 s, 3–5 numbered reasons with one
   real study each, a twist near the end, one-line takeaway. Elements land 0.25 s before the
   word (this project's pipeline does that automatically).
4. **Packaging before production.** Write the title and sketch the thumbnail *before* the script.
   Thumbnail: the stickman doing the emotion + 1–3 huge words in red ("NEVER SORRY"), paper
   background. Title ≤ 55 characters, curiosity + "you" ("Why Most People Never Say Sorry").
   Make 2–3 thumbnails and use YouTube's Test & Compare.
5. **Shorts as the funnel.** Cut every long video into 3–5 vertical Shorts (one reason each,
   30–50 s). Same look. End each Short on the open question the long video answers.
6. **Cadence.** One long video a week beats two rushed ones. With this pipeline a video is:
   script (1 day) → record VO (1 hr) → `npm run build` + tweak cues (half day) → render.
7. **Read the numbers every Sunday.** CTR < 4% → fix title/thumbnail; average view duration < 35%
   → fix the first 60 s; a video doing 3× your average → make two more on the same feeling.
8. **Series and playlists.** "Psychology of Relationships", "Why People Do That", "Dark
   Psychology (and how to spot it)". Playlists raise session time; end screens point to the next
   video in the series.
9. **Community.** Pin a question in comments ("When did you last refuse to apologise?"), reply to
   the first hour of comments, and turn the best comments into future topics.
10. **Monetise (1k subs + 4k watch hours, or 10M Shorts views).** Ads first; then a free
    "cheat-sheet" PDF per video for an email list; later sponsors (BetterHelp-type apps are the
    usual fit for this niche — vet them), and a paid course or book once you have ~50k.

## Rules that protect growth

- Cite real research on screen (author + year) and never invent numbers. In psychology the
  audience fact-checks you in the comments, and trust is the moat.
- Same visual system every time; change the topic, never the look.
- No clickbait the video doesn't pay off: it kills retention, and retention drives
  recommendations.
